# MIT-07/08: Dynamic likelihood quotients and robust instrument transfer

Draft 0.1, 2026-11-25. Evidence: algebraic arguments for a declared finite model and exact finite checks. No new Lean proof or neural proof-task run. The deterministic precursor is MIT-05.

## Model and exact quotient

One hidden winning strategy i has prior weight w_i >= 0 (positive total). A guess costs one and perfectly identifies success or failure; failure eliminates that strategy. Instrument j costs a positive integer c_j and returns a finite outcome y according to L_j(y|i). Instrument outcomes are conditionally independent given i and each instrument can be queried at most once. Budget B is a nonnegative integer. No other observations or constraints are modeled. A query leaving no budget for a guess can be omitted without changing the optimum.

At a history of nonterminal observations let u_i denote unnormalized posterior mass, and A the unused instruments. Define a class by the pair (u_i, (L_j(.|i))_{j in A}); keep its count n and omit zero-mass classes. All guesses of members of one class give success mass u and decrement n on the failure branch. A query j partitions by outcomes: multiply the per-member mass by L_j(y), remove column j and re-coalesce classes with identical remaining signatures and updated mass. Branch values are summed (using unnormalized weights). A direct guess list takes the B largest per-member masses with multiplicity.

**Exactness.** Induct on remaining budget. Terminal cases are zero and empty support. For each feasible first guess, its immediate hit and the compressed failure state depend only on the guessed class. For each feasible first query, all successor class multisets depend only on the current class multiset and the outcome. Hence both expanded and compressed Bellman recurrences maximize the same finite action values by induction. Randomized actions cannot exceed the maximum. Projection and coalescence are valid even when two previously unequal masses become equal after Bayes weighting. Constant response distributions across live classes are pure noise; such a query can be replaced by a mixture of continuation policies without its cost, so skipping it preserves the optimum. Static comparator retains all signature columns, marking consumed queries unaffordable.

The result depends on known conditional likelihoods and exchangeable guess actions within a class. With correlated responses conditioned on the winner alone, a history-dependent likelihood model or expanded latent state is necessary. This does not formalize arbitrary mathematical proofs or establish neural transfer.

## Robustness under misspecification

Compare true and approximate channels on identical strategy labels, query identities, outcome alphabets and costs. Normalize their priors; let d be their total variation distance. For each instrument let e_j be the maximum over strategy labels of the total variation distance between its true and approximate outcome laws. For B>=1 let R(A,b) be the minimum of products of (1-e_j) over subsets of A with total cost <= b, including the empty set. Put D=1-(1-d)*R(all,B-1). For B=0 both optimal values and regret are zero.

**Fixed-policy bound.** For any shared legal policy that omits queries leaving no budget for a guess, couple the hidden strategies maximally (match probability 1-d). Couple internal random choices identically. On matching histories and hidden strategy, guesses have identical feedback; a query j can be coupled maximally with match probability at least 1-e_j. By induction on remaining budget b, conditional on a match so far, the chance of keeping the two transcripts matched is at least R(A,max(0,b-1)): for a guess the remaining feasible subsets shrink; for a query j the induction bound is multiplied by (1-e_j), and the product corresponds to a feasible subset before the query. The common-policy restriction prevents spending the last unit on a useless query; each query is once-only. Thus the probability of any disagreement is at most D. Identical coupled transcripts give identical closure, proving |V_true(pi)-V_approx(pi)|<=D. Maximizing over the same policy class gives |V_true*-V_approx*|<=D. With pi_hat approximate-optimal, add and subtract its approximate value: V_true*-V_true(pi_hat)<=2D, also <=1. Histories impossible under the approximate model require a specified legal extension; the bound holds for any such extension. Product minimization is a 0/1 multiplicative knapsack, not an independent additive-error heuristic. The certificate can be loose; it is worst-case over strategies and feasible query subsets.

**Sharp factor of two.** Budget one, two labels, no queries. True prior (2/5,3/5), approximate (1/2,1/2); tie-break on first label. D=1/10, actual closure 2/5, true optimum 3/5, regret 1/5=2D. No query-error sharpness claim.

**Equal predicted value, unequal realized policy:** eight equal-prior labels, one unit-cost binary query, budget three. Four labels mostly return y=0 and four mostly y=1; within each side two have confidence h and two l<h with l>=3/4 and h>3/4. Approximation swaps high and low ranks within each side. Both optima are h/2 (direct guesses give 3/8); approximate policy deployed under truth gives l/2. Thus identical predicted optimal closure can hide regret (h-l)/2. Sixteen deliberately designed seeds permute labels and choose h and l. All 16 exhibit regret; mean 13/320 and max 1/20. In the 32 random small perturbation roots the deployed regret was zero; these small fixtures often favor direct search and are weak discrimination tests. Neither set is a population sample or a sealed empirical test.
