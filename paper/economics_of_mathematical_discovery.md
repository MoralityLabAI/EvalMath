# The Economics of Mathematical Discovery: Verified Abstractions, Information Acquisition, and Compact Reasoners

Draft of 2026-11-25. Repository: MoralityLabAI/EvalMath, research branch `research/vad01-verified-abstraction-20261125` (PR 72). Every numerical claim below is traceable to a committed receipt, note or test listed in the claim registry (`output/verified_abstraction/claim_registry.json`) and Appendix C, except the two cited web sources, which are not archived in the repository and are marked level C. Figures regenerate with `scripts/make_vad_figures.py`.

## Abstract

Recent public reports describe automated systems producing libraries of verified intermediate results far larger than the theorems that motivated them. We ask when such abstractions pay for themselves, and how an agent should spend a finite budget when what it learns is information as well as proofs. We formalize a verified library as a cache with a charged discovery cost, derive exact amortization and break-even laws, show that lemma value is non-submodular (complementary lemmas exist), and measure the effect of distribution shift, validation and unreliable cues on discovery. We then treat research selection as stochastic control over a dependency hypergraph with an exact finite oracle, and separate immediate, reusable and informational value. All results are finite or synthetic: exact theorems about stylised models, exactly enumerated examples, seeded simulations, Lean-kernel-verified instances of small proof families, and negative results for small recurrent controllers, which failed the gate we applied against symbolic baselines (the gate rule was fixed in our work plan before training but was first committed together with the results). We state what the two public reports that motivated the work do and do not establish for our models, give an evidence-level registry for every claim, and report the independent referee review that found weak points in an earlier draft, with our responses.

## 1. Motivation and relation to the primary sources

Two public reports in autumn 2026 frame the problem. We read the primary pages (retrieved 2026-11-25; no snapshot is archived in this repository) and report only what they state; both are vendor reports and are cited, not verified.

* **Anthropic, "Formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem" (4 Sep 2026).** The page claims an end-to-end Lean formalization of the general theorem (no positive integers a,b,c with a^n+b^n=c^n for n>2), about 13 million lines and 30,300 theorems of which 29,500 are used, produced in 11 days by dozens of Claude agents with about six billion output tokens, using Lean's standard axioms, with a comparator check of the statement against Mathlib's formulation and review by Kevin Buzzard.
* **OpenAI, "Building scientific understanding with academic communities" (page dated 26 Oct 2026).** The page claims models proved the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, with Kakeya and Stein restriction following, via a "novel auxiliary partial differential equation"; the model output is dated 18 Oct and a Lean formalization the next day; an unnamed expert recommended release; no peer review is stated. The accompanying repository lists the Lean-covered statements. We did not inspect the Lean files, their axioms, or whether the formal statements match the conjectures, and found no statement by the specialists, no refereeing, and no independent formal check.

**Relationship to this paper.** Both reports are cited upstream claims that we neither reproduce nor audit. They motivate three questions that a synthetic, fully checkable setting can answer exactly: how much computation does a reusable verified abstraction save and what does it cost to find; how does that depend on what the abstraction is validated against; and how should discovery effort be allocated when intermediate results carry information as well as proofs. We make no claim about the mathematics of either report, about how either system chose lemmas, or that our synthetic gains transfer to real libraries. In particular the Anthropic scale figures do not calibrate any parameter here.

## 2. Framework and exact results

Notation: a *library* L is a set of verified propositions; a *target* is a proposition to be proved; the cost of a proof is its reference count under a fixed grammar. Unless a statement says "theorem" or "identity" it is a finite computation.

### 2.1 Amortized abstraction utility (VAD-01, 05, 06)

For a cache a (a verified lemma), target t has cost C_a(t) <= C_0(t). For deployment horizon H targets drawn from a family, discovery work D, and construction cost K(a), the net gain is

    G(H) = sum_{i<=H} (C_0(t_i) - C_a(t_i)) - D - K(a),

and an abstraction pays only when H exceeds the break-even horizon H* = (D+K)/mean-saving. In the GF(2) cache model the saving is exactly Delta(t,a)=2 max(0, 2|supp t ∩ supp a| - |a| - 1) (VAD-01A, algebraic identity, 4,096 checks). In the grammar-restricted Lean conjunction gym (VAD-05A) the direct proof wins at H=8 (4,626 versus 5,898 for shape generalization once discovery and construction are charged) and the shape cache wins by 1.0959x at H=256 under a frozen eight-target selection (exploratory, nested prefixes). In the Horn-clause gym (VAD-06A) structural support selection saves 4.75% at H=256 (support 133,164 versus direct 139,811 reference units), profitably in all 24 shared and mixed families and unprofitably in the 12 shifted families.

### 2.2 Complementary lemmas (VAD-02, VAD-10)

Savings are monotone but not submodular: for GF(2) vectors t=7, a=25, b=30, C_∅(t)=C_{a}(t)=C_{b}(t)=6 and C_{a,b}(t)=4, so b's marginal saving rises from 0 to 2 once a is present (exact, by BFS). Construction order also matters: F({7,15})=10 against 14 built independently. In the agenda model of §2.7 the same phenomenon appears as a value-of-research law: two lemmas that each cost 8 to derive, with deployment giving up at cost cap 10, add nothing alone and 18 utility units together; any controller that scores a lemma by its own marginal utility stops (greedy-0 = -30), a one-step lookahead recovers the optimum (-12).

### 2.3 Distribution shift, validation, invention (VAD-03 to 06)

Abstractions selected on development families fail when deployment differs. In VAD-04A (synthetic GF(2), 336 test families) an intersection-invention arm without access to the planted pair attains 1.199x over direct proof, is profitable in 238/240 constructive families and 0/96 null or shifted ones. In Horn families a frozen support-selected cache loses in every shifted family (VAD-06A: gains 0.81 shifted versus 1.72 shared), a charged validation slice rejects 24/24 immediate-shift and dominated candidates but accepts 24/24 late-shift candidates with zero savings (VAD-06B), and dominance screening fails its gate because dominated alternate routes expose hubs (VAD-06D). Bounded ancestor indexing recovers latent and concealed unlocks (VAD-06D2: 1.605x, 1.155x after charging a common deployment-DP scan). These are synthetic mechanism results on public seeds without third-party sealing.

### 2.4 Optimal stopping with fallible cues (VAD-07)

For latent types with priors w_i, discovery times t_i and iid cues with positive rate p_i, the sufficient statistic of a search record is (n,k) and the value satisfies V(n,k)=max(0,-c+Σ...) (exact Bellman recursion in Fractions; note VAD-07). The VAD-07 note proves an equivalence between fixed cutoffs and threshold policies on (n,k) in the stated model; we cite it without re-deriving it. A minimax argument over unreliable cue channels is immediate when the protected set contains the blind channel (07B). In matched Horn prefixes a cue-conditioned cutoff reaches 1.2537x over the direct baseline (1.0863x after a common deployment-DP scan charge; 13.4% below the best fixed-cap comparator) under regime weights that correlate the cue with future utility; under cue-shift and uniform mixtures the fixed policies win.

### 2.5 Online reliability calibration (VAD-08)

With latent mode R in {0,1} (cues reliable or reversed) and censored records (n,k,h), the exact likelihood L_r(n,k,h)=Σ_t w_t q_{r,t}^k(1-q_{r,t})^(f-k) 1[...] makes Bayesian filtering exact for the declared model; a mismatch monitor with a likelihood-ratio martingale bounds false alarms by 1/20 (Ville's inequality, in exact arithmetic) and fires on weak-cue streams 32/32, on joint shifts 29/32 and on Markov streams 1/32. A 369-parameter GRU failed the advancement gate (VAD-08 receipt).

### 2.6 Cross-family value of information (VAD-09, 09D)

Carrying the reliability mode across families, the exact finite-menu optimum satisfies V_h(p)=max_a[(1-p)r_0(a)+p r_1(a)+Σ_o z V_{h-1}(q)] with q=η+(1-2η)p l_1/z (hazard η), and V_h is convex piecewise affine, so the backup is computed exactly by sweeping rational breakpoints without discretizing beliefs. Reproduced exactly (this work, byte-identical receipts for 09A and 09C): optimal utilities 9.3777, 20.8631, 37.8795 for one, two and three families; at horizon three, the first action cut24 sacrifices 7.078 immediate utility (2.3 versus 9.3777) and improves the total by 1.8981 over a myopic first action followed by the optimum. For 96 families the long-horizon policy beats myopic by 8.344 total (0.49%, 0.0869 per family) with the Bayes hazard correctly specified; with the planner assuming hazard 0.05 and the true hazard 0.25 or 0.5 the long-horizon policy is worse than myopic (965.8 versus 982.1; 465.1 versus 495.2). A 401-point grid simulation over 24,576 families found no reliable advantage of 32-family planning over one-step planning (+0.013, interval [-0.174, 0.211]) and the practical gate fails. §3.1 adds the 09D decomposition of mode identification versus law learning.

### 2.7 Research agenda selection (VAD-10)

The agent holds a verified library L, a posterior π over unknown hyperedge existence, and budget b; V(L,π,b) is defined by exact recursion over attempts, experiments and stopping, with terminal value the expected cost of N future problems. Proposition 1 (exact identity) splits any action's value into immediate reward, reusable library change, and information gain in two telescoping orders. Proposition 2 (theorem): when edges leave the facts independently, have distinct conclusions and equal costs, greedy one-step net gain is optimal. Dropping hypotheses breaks it as follows (all exact): unequal costs (knapsack) give greedy 2 versus 3; complementary lemmas, which violate the premises-among-facts structure, give -30 versus -12; a chain of k edges with end-only payoff needs lookahead k-1 (Proposition 3, with ties broken toward stopping, lambda=0 and unit future problem count); correlated edges in the layered sweep make greedy-0 optimal in 70% of 60 seeded instances (78.7% of 150 uncorrelated layered instances, so that sweep does not isolate independence; a referee-supplied modular two-regime test found greedy-0 suboptimal in 46 of 1,500 seeds, not committed as a receipt); and a misleading heuristic signal reverses the value of trusting feedback (trusting beats ignoring only when the true reliability is at least 11/20). We found no counterexample for dropping 'distinct conclusions' or 'no experiments' in 5,500 random trials (referee search, not committed), and do not claim those hypotheses are necessary. In a strategic regime the best research action is a zero-reward lemma: at P(direct)=1/5 the optimum -18 uses lemma M first while direct pursuit of the highest-reward target yields -23.44; M is the optimal first action for every direct-route probability up to 31/40 in a 40-point scan and the direct edge from 4/5 on; at probability 1 the optimal policy takes the target first and then M (-14.8), whereas the goal-directed baseline stops after the target (-18), so the baseline's loss is the forgone reuse value rather than a wrong first action.

## 3. Experiments added in this work

### 3.1 VAD-09D: mode identification versus law learning (E)

*Model.* Latent state (mode m, law l): mode 0 is the nominal cue channel, mode 1 the reversed channel; the mode flips with hazard 1/20 per family; the law is either nominal or weak (every cue probability moved halfway to 1/2) and never changes. Seven VAD-09A actions (cutoffs 0, 2, 8, 24 and Bayes plans p0, p50, p100) plus two priced probes that take the safe cutoff-2 action, pay one utility unit, and reveal the current mode or the law exactly. Three families, prior probability 1/2 on reversed mode and on weak law; exact Fractions, 119,709 Q evaluations for the optimum (`scripts/verify_vad_voi.py`, receipt `vad_09d.json`, two tests).

*Results (expected total utility over three families).* Blind best fixed action 26.700; myopic Bayesian filter over the four-state belief 27.295 (it never uses a probe); finite-horizon optimum without probes 31.255; finite-horizon optimum with probes 40.181, whose first action is the mode probe, and receding-horizon (h=2) control attains exactly the same value; a privileged oracle that sees the mode every family 53.430, identical whether or not the law is also known. Decomposition (receipt `vad_09d_attribution.json`): planning depth without probes is worth 3.96 over the myopic filter; the explicit mode probe adds 8.93 on top of the planner (so 8.93 of the 12.89 gap between the probe-using optimum and the myopic filter belongs to the information action, not to planning depth); knowing the law adds 0.97 to the no-probe planner but only 0.003 to the probe-using planner. A heuristic that probes the mode once and then filters myopically attains exactly the probe-using optimum, 40.181 (receipt `vad_09d_heuristic.json`; probing the law first instead gives 27.06), so planning depth adds nothing once the probe is taken. The mode oracle is worth 22.2 over the best no-probe planner and 13.25 over the planner that may probe. Hence (i) the explicit information action pays where implicit exploration inside a within-family policy did not (VAD-09B failed its gate); (ii) knowing the mode matters and knowing the law does not once the mode is known, because in this menu the best action for a given mode is the same under both laws; the law matters only through how fast cues reveal the mode; (iii) consequently the 12.89 advantage of the probe-using optimum over myopic filtering is the probe's value and not the value of planning; the allowance for planning overhead in this model is that of the no-probe planner, 3.96 over three families, i.e. planning is worth paying for only while one Q-evaluation costs less than about 3.3e-5 utility (taking the 119,709 evaluations of the full solve as an upper bound on work; hazard assumed correct), and zero planning is needed once an exact mode probe is available. The 96-family grid result of section 2.6 gives 0.0869 utility per family as the corresponding allowance without probes. The mode probe is valuable here because the model prices it at one unit while the oracle gap is 22; this says nothing about the cost of such a probe in real discovery.

*Not covered.* Correlated discovery times across families, null-prevalence shifts and abrupt change points are modelled only in the 09B sampled stress test (no reliable advantage) and in the hazard-misspecification table of section 2.6; they are not in the exact 09D model.

*Probe pricing (companion receipt `vad_09d_probes.json`).* With the mode probe priced at 0.5, 1, 2, 4 or 6 utility units the finite-horizon optimum (two families) uses it every time and its value falls exactly one unit per unit of price, from +6.58 to +1.08 over the no-probe optimum; the probe stops paying at a price of about 7.08 (two families). The law probe is never chosen at any price: the optimum with the law probe equals the optimum without it. Three families on a four-action menu (cutoff 2, p0, p50, p100) give the same ordering: mode probe +10.10 at price 1 (+8.10 at price 3), break-even price about 11.10, law probe worth exactly zero. Since a mode probe in this model returns the current mode exactly, these are upper bounds on the value of a noisy or delayed one.

### 3.2 VAD-11: a harder Lean gym with measured costs

*Construction.* Five fresh theorem families in core Lean 4 (no Mathlib): F1 Boolean polynomial identities over 4-7 variables, F2 modular arithmetic `P(n) % m` claims for polynomial P, F3 finite algebra on `Fin m`, F4 graph invariants on 5-7 vertex graphs given as adjacency matrices, F5 Nat inequality chains. Each family has at least two competing proof strategies for the same statement (brute-force `decide`, composition of a library lemma, and for F5 direct and `omega`), a reusable library of kernel-checked lemma units, a seeded generator, a template renderer and a separately written (same team) re-printer/parser that must agree byte for byte, and a Python semantic evaluator that checks every statement true by finite enumeration (3,000 statements, 3,000/3,000 agree). Calibration (seeds 81000-81199) and test (82000-82399) splits are frozen with disjoint templates, moduli (F2 calibration {3,4,6,8,10,12} against test {5,7,9,11}) and sizes (F3, F4); a hashed manifest covers all statements. A transitive dependency auditor (a parser over the generated sources, not a general Lean dependency tool) over 2,101 declarations rejects any lemma that reaches a held-out target or descendant; the 15 rejections are exactly the deliberately planted leak controls, and all ten mutation controls behave as designed.

*Kernel closure and costs (K, E).* 200 instances (20 per family per split) times every strategy were compiled in fresh processes under both Lean 4.19.0 and 4.35.0: 536 verifier calls per toolchain, 0 failures, no `sorry`; axiom reports are none (394), `propext` (346) or `propext` plus `Quot.sound` (140), never `Classical.choice`. Per-file in-Lean work, peak RSS (about 260 MB, dominated by the Lean runtime) and wall time are recorded. Median in-Lean work (ms, Lean 4.35.0): F1 decide 59.7 versus lemma 46.1; F2 106.5 versus 44.1; F3 111.5 versus 212.2; F4 344.5 versus 218.7; F5 direct 34.1, omega 60.5, lemma 31.9. Wall time per file (about 2 s) is process start-up and is nearly strategy independent, so verifier-call count rather than work_ms is the dominant physical cost at these sizes. **The gym is therefore a modest test of abstraction value: under Lean 4.35.0 the medians of the two main strategies differ by factors of 1.0 to 2.4 in work (under 4.19.0 up to 4.2x for F1), F3's lemma route loses at small sizes, and we did not measure `decide` scaling beyond the sampled sizes.**

*Abstraction invention.* Structural anti-unification (a least-general generalization of statement trees) produces candidate lemmas for F1, F2 and F5 only (not applicable to F3, F4). All honest candidates were accepted and kernel-checked; the leaky controls were rejected by the auditor. Invented lemmas hit 75/200 (F1), 13/200 (F2) and 84/200 (F5) calibration targets but **0/400 and 0/400 fresh F1 and F2 test targets**, and 62/400 for F5 (15.5% against 42% on calibration). The F1 and F2 zeros are forced by the template-disjoint split and are not evidence about the method; the informative number is that a leaky control lemma, invented from test instances, hits 170/400 F1 test targets and is rejected by the dependency auditor.

*Measured research-selection economics (VAD-11E, replay; E/S).* The replay uses the recorded in-Lean work of the 20 frozen test targets per family (`work_ms`: elaboration and type-checking, excluding process start-up and import time) and the same measure for library units. Under Lean 4.35.0 the total relative to proving every target directly is: best searched prebuilt set 1.331x, Bayesian online building 1.259x, greedy-0 calibration selection 1.195x, support-graph selection 1.128x, lookahead-2/3 1.102x, on-demand retrieval 0.492x. **These ratios are not robust and should not be read as an economic gain.** (a) Under Lean 4.19.0 the same receipt gives 1.087 (Bayes), 1.101 (greedy-0), 1.016 (lookahead) and 1.270 (searched set). (b) Charging per-file wall time, which includes the roughly 2 s process start-up and charges each library unit as its own verifier call, every policy is between 0.72x and 1.05x (Bayes 1.000, greedy-0 1.014, support 0.834, retrieval 0.722 under 4.35.0), i.e. abstraction does not pay at a 20-target horizon in this gym once verifier calls are counted. (c) Calibration and test use disjoint templates by design and the lemma units used by calibration targets overlap test targets in only 0/6 (F1), 1/6 (F2), 1/13 (F3), 4/4 (F4) and 2/6 (F5) units, so any set chosen on calibration cannot help in F1, F2, F3 and F5, and the pooled gain of the calibration-selected policies is essentially family F4; they do not measure the quality of the selection rules. (d) Pooling is by raw time and F3 and F4 are 89% of the direct total; per family, on-demand retrieval is as good as or better than the Bayesian rule in F1, F2, F4 and F5 and loses only in F3, where the break-even horizon (91) exceeds the session. (e) The library is written by the authors, covers the test templates at no discovery cost, and the invention method above does not reproduce that coverage; the best searched prebuilt set is a size-limited subset search, not a proven optimum (retrieval beats it in F1). (f) One 20-target session per family gives no interval and there is no horizon sweep, so the break-even horizons (F1 8.9, F2 7.1, F3 91, F4 2.5, F5 15.6, computed as total build cost over mean per-target saving) are extrapolations from heavy-tailed samples and are lower bounds because library discovery cost is zero. What the replay does show is the direction of the amortization law with measured kernel work and its sensitivity to what is counted.

*Physical follow-up (VAD-13B/13C; added to this branch by a parallel session after this draft, read here from its committed notes and not re-executed or audited by the author of this section).* Matched direct-versus-lemma batch timings on GitHub Actions runners, both Lean toolchains, generation time included, three repetitions, horizons 8 and 32, with the strategy per family chosen on a calibration block (`paper/verified_abstraction_calibrated_results.md`, run 45583084890). The pooled ratio of calibration-selected to direct total seconds is 1.075 (4.19) and 1.118 (4.35) at H=32, and 0.430 and 1.060 at H=8; always-lemma gives 1.074 and 0.916 at H=32. F1, F2 and F4 favour the lemma route at H=32, F5 favours direct, and F3 favours direct on 4.35 and selects the slower route on 4.19. Charging calibration executions, the estimated break-even is 31 (4.19) and 21 (4.35) sessions of 32 targets. These numbers agree in direction with the sensitivity analysis above: wall-time-inclusive gains are small, family dependent and negative at short horizons, and the 1.26x of the work-only replay is not an economic result. They remain descriptive (one test block per family and version, pooled medians from different runner jobs, authored library).

### 3.3 VAD-12: neural advancement gate (N, negative)

*Protocol.* Weight-tied slot-refinement controllers (about 12.2k parameters: `trm` with episode GRU, `memoryless`, `fixed_depth` K=1, flat `ff` MLP, `trm_oracle_features`) were trained on 6,000 exact-oracle decision sequences (train seeds 90000-90999 across layered, modular and correlated families; mixture of optimal, greedy-0 and random behavior) with calibration seeds 91000-91249, 25 epochs, two seeds, on one CPU thread. Test families were generated after training: 100 in-distribution instances and 60 each of three structurally fresh families (wide graphs, planted complementary pairs, planted delayed-payoff chains), all scored by exact forward evaluation against the exact optimum. Because greedy-2 is exactly optimal on these small instances the gate amounts to requiring optimality, and the fresh families (chains of length three) were written after Proposition 3, so this is a stringent contest rather than a fair one. The training code lives in MoralityLabAI/LoopedTransformers (`experiments/vad12_agenda_controller`, main commit 181bb78c); EvalMath owns the instances, oracle and scoring.

*Result.* Mean regret (utility units, lower is better), in-distribution / wide / complementary / chain: greedy-2 0 / 0 / 0 / 0; greedy-1 0 / 0.001 / 0.034 / 2.41; greedy-0 0.119 / 0.118 / 0.189 / 2.25; direct 0.404 / 0.465 / 4.29 / 0.169; `trm` 0.114 / 0.114 / 0.889 / 0.352; `memoryless` 0.076 / 0.137 / 0.811 / 0.424; `fixed_depth` 0.091 / 0.199 / 0.873 / 0.308; `ff` 0.283 / 0.632 / 1.90 / 1.35; `trm_oracle_features` 0.064 / 0.146 / 0.501 / 0.417. The gate we applied (neural regret within 0.01 of greedy-2 on every fresh family at lower measured cost; the rule was fixed in our work plan before training but first committed with the results) **fails for every arm**. Neural arms beat the weak baselines only in places: they beat greedy-0 by 0.005 to 0.055 in distribution and on wide graphs (arm-dependent), beat `direct` on complementary instances (0.5-1.9 versus 4.29), and on chains beat greedy-0 and greedy-1 (Proposition 3: a chain needs lookahead k-1) with paired intervals excluding zero (for example `trm` minus greedy-1 -2.06, bootstrap interval [-3.03, -1.15]); but on chains the goal-directed `direct` baseline (0.169) beats every neural arm, and no neural arm approaches greedy-2 anywhere. Shuffled-memory and memoryless arms are indistinguishable from `trm` at two seeds (seed spread is as large as the differences, for example `trm` in-distribution 0.066 and 0.162, chain 0.582 and 0.122), so neither episode memory nor weight-tied recurrence measurably helped. We cannot attribute this to the problem being Markov: the controllers see per-edge marginal probabilities, costs, rewards, future mass, verified flags, four static graph features and the budget, not the posterior over worlds, N, the cap or experiment likelihoods, so the features are not a sufficient statistic and the failure is confounded with feature insufficiency, with training on only 1,000 instances (each rolled out six times), with labels that break ties toward stopping, and with checkpoint selection by action accuracy rather than regret (`ff` has the highest accuracy and the worst regret). The oracle-feature arm (it also receives greedy-0's one-step net gain) still has higher regret than greedy-0 on wide and complementary instances, which points to the training recipe rather than missing information. A known flaw: the shuffled-memory ablation substitutes the state after step t where the state entering step t was intended; the models were not saved so it could not be re-run. Cost: about 3.9 ms per neural decision in unoptimized Python (feature construction included), against 14-159 ms per instance for greedy-1/2, whose evaluator walks a tree of about 7-9 decision nodes; per instance the neural controllers (about 22-46 ms) are therefore not cheaper than greedy-1/2 on the complementary and chain families, and the comparison is not like-for-like. Training took 17 s (`ff`) to 444 s (`memoryless`) per model on a loaded CPU. Because the gate failed, nothing was scaled to the local GPU. No claim is made about larger models, learned recalibration (not implemented in this first gate), or raw-history inputs where memory could matter.

## 4. Integrated account

(1) *Abstractions amortize only past a break-even, and only under matched distributions.* In the cost models tried (GF(2) caches, conjunction and Horn gyms, measured Lean work) a cached verified lemma loses at short horizons and can win at long ones when deployment resembles development; under shift it loses at every horizon (VAD-06A shifted 0.81x at H=256, VAD-04A 0/96 null or shifted families profitable). Computed break-even horizons on the kernel-checked gym range from 2.5 to 91 targets but no horizon above 20 was measured and, counting verifier calls, no policy beat direct proof at 20 targets (section 3.2). (2) *Value is not additive.* Savings are monotone and non-submodular (VAD-02) and the same complementarity defeats myopic research selection (VAD-10): greedy scoring is optimal only on a narrow class (Proposition 2) and fails for unequal costs, complementary lemmas, delayed payoff and correlated structure; one or two steps of lookahead repair almost all finite cases. (3) *Value is distribution dependent.* Selection on development families loses on shifted families, a charged validation slice protects against immediate shift but not late shift, and anti-unification on the new gym finds lemmas that do not transfer to fresh templates. (4) *Information is a separate asset.* Fallible cues need stopping rules (VAD-07) and online calibration (VAD-08); across families the exact optimum can pay an immediate cost to learn the mode (VAD-09), but the implicit exploration available to a within-family policy produced no reliable practical gain, whereas an explicit, cheap, exact mode probe did (VAD-09D) because the mode, not the observation law, is what the best action depends on. (5) *Allocation is the unifying problem.* VAD-10 expresses the immediate, reusable and informational parts of a research action in one exact identity, shows a zero-reward lemma can be the best action, and supplies the symbolic comparators any learned selector must beat. (6) *Compact learned controllers have not earned a place.* The VAD-12 slot-recurrent controllers failed the gate against greedy-2, with recurrence and memory ablations indistinguishable from the full model at two seeds; the VAD-03B and VAD-08 GRU failures are reported from notes whose neural receipts could not be reproduced from the repository (they need torch and an external bundle, per the reproduction audit). On the VAD-12 instances the exact lookahead-2 baseline already attains the optimum. Given the limits listed in section 3.3 (lossy features, 1,000 training instances, two seeds), this is evidence that a small imitation learner on these features does not beat exact lookahead on tiny instances, not evidence about regimes where exact lookahead is infeasible.

Evidence mix: theorems and identities (Propositions 1-3, VAD-01 and VAD-07 recursions), exactly enumerated examples and oracles (VAD-02, 09A, 09D, 10), synthetic mechanism results (VAD-03 to 06, 09B, 11E), kernel-verified instances (VAD-05 and 06: the CI lean job succeeded on the PR head, while the committed receipts keep kernel NOT_RUN; VAD-11: 200 instances under two toolchains, locally replayed), numerical approximations (VAD-09B and 09C grids) and neural findings (all negative).

## 4a. Evidence-level taxonomy

We use five levels in decreasing strength of claim: **T** exact theorem or algebraic identity about a stated model; **E** finite exactly enumerated example or exact oracle output; **S** synthetic mechanism result (seeded simulation or finite family sweep under declared generators); **K** instance kernel-verified in Lean (a statement about the checked instance only); **N** numerical approximation or neural finding. Every claim in the registry carries one of these.

## 5. Limitations

All generators are authored by the research team; public seeds and nested horizons make confirmatory interpretation impossible; second implementations (the naive evaluator, the re-printers, the semantic evaluator and the replay) are same-team re-implementations of a shared model and check code, not model validity; only the Lean kernel check is independent of our code, and only for the checked instance; costs are abstract reference counts, not physical compute, with measured Lean times only in §3.2; no experiment uses real research data; the neural controllers are tiny, trained on exact-oracle labels from 1,000 instances of three families and tested on structurally fresh ones, with lossy features, so their failure bounds nothing about large models.

## 6. Next experiment

A defensible next DataGoblin/TRM training experiment is not a bigger copy of VAD-12. It should (a) move to instances too large for exact lookahead (n >= 12 vertices, correlated worlds in factored form), so a learned controller has something to save; (b) withhold the exact belief from the inputs and supply only observation history, so memory is necessary and the shuffled-memory ablation can bite; (c) include a misleading-signal family with an explicit recalibration action; (d) use the VAD-11 kernel gym with measured per-target costs as the environment rather than the abstract reference model; and (e) pre-declare the gate: regret within 0.01 of greedy-2 (or of a sampled-lookahead comparator where exact greedy-2 is infeasible) on at least 300 fresh instances per family at five or more times lower measured decision cost, with a paired interval excluding zero, then scale to the RTX 5080 only on a pass. The VAD-12 recipe record (`output/verified_abstraction/datagoblin/vad12_data_recipe.json`) is the template.

## Appendix A. Proofs

**Proposition 1.** For action a at state (L,pi,b) with continuation budget b' write W(L,pi) = V(L,pi,b'). Then Q(a) = sum_y P(y)[u(a,y) + W(L'_y,pi'_y)]. Add and subtract W(L,pi) and W(L'_y,pi) inside the sum: the terms are W(L,pi), E[u], E[W(L',pi) - W(L,pi)] and E[W(L',pi') - W(L',pi)], which sum to Q(a) by telescoping. The same holds with W(L,pi') in place of W(L',pi). Both are asserted in `Oracle.decompose` on every call.

**Proposition 2.** Setup as in section 2.7. Let e_v be the unique edge into v, premises among the facts, with probability p_v. T is a sum over vertices of -N mu_v D_v, and D_v depends only on whether v is verified and whether e_v exists; so verifying v by a successful attempt changes T by N mu_v (D_v^0 - 1), where D_v^0 = min(cap, c + |premises|) is the cost with e_v present and v unverified. An attempt costs c, succeeds with probability p_v, and leaves every other vertex's value and posterior untouched (independence). Hence V(L,pi,b) = T(L,pi) + the maximum, over subsets S of unverified attemptable vertices with |S| c <= b, of the sum of u_v, where u_v = p_v (r_v + N mu_v (D_v^0 - 1)) - lambda c, and attempting one vertex never changes any other u_w. Taking the floor(b/c) largest positive u_v is optimal (exchange argument for a sum of independent terms under a cardinality constraint), and greedy-0 selects at every step the vertex with the largest Q(a) - T = u_v. QED.

**Proposition 3.** (Conditions: lambda = 0, N = 1 with all future mass on a fact, budget at least k, p and R positive, ties between equal-value actions broken toward stopping; with ties broken toward attempting, lower-depth greedy also attains V.) In a chain of k edges with success probability p each and reward R only at the end, any action sequence either completes the chain (probability p^k, reward R, spending k budget units) or stops after a failure; T is constant. Hence V = p^k R + T_0 when p^k R > 0, where T_0 = -1 is the stop value, and the value of attempting the first edge followed by at most j further actions equals T_0 unless j >= k-1 (otherwise the reward cannot be reached). Thus greedy-j attains V iff j >= k-1.

**Statement on 09A.** The upper-hull representation and the exactness of the rational envelope backup are proved in the VAD-09 note; this paper relies on that note and on the replayed receipts rather than re-deriving them.
