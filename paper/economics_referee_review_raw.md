## 1. Summary

The paper formalises a verified library as a cache with charged discovery cost (exact break-even laws), shows lemma value is non-submodular, studies distribution shift and cue reliability on synthetic gyms, builds an exact finite oracle for research-agenda selection (VAD-10, Propositions 1-3), adds an exact value-of-information model (VAD-09D), a five-family kernel-checked Lean gym with measured costs and a policy replay (VAD-11/11E), and a negative neural-controller gate (VAD-12). The scope hedging is mostly honest (synthetic, same-author, public seeds), the registry is a good device, and most headline numbers reproduce from the receipts. The weak points are (i) the headline VAD-11E economics, which rest on a cost definition that excludes the dominant physical cost, a single toolchain, and a calibration/test design that makes calibration-selected policies unable to win by construction; (ii) the VAD-12 reading, which says the exact belief is supplied and the problem is Markov when only per-edge marginals plus a few static features are supplied, and which contains one false comparative sentence; (iii) a handful of overstated "necessity", "independent", "pre-declared" and "wins at long horizons" claims; (iv) several small arithmetic or attribution slips. Recommendation: **revise (major)**.

### M2. The replay policy comparison (n = 5 single sessions) says little about the policies (OVERSTATEMENT)
Sentence: "greedy-0 calibration selection 1.195x, support-graph selection 1.128x, lookahead-2/3 1.102x, on-demand retrieval 0.492x (it builds every needed lemma and loses)."
- Calibration and test use disjoint templates by design. Counting `S_lemma.imports` in the receipt (4.35.0): F1 calibration lemmas (10) and test lemmas (6) overlap in **0**; F2 1 of 6; F3 1 of 13; F5 2 of 6; only F4 overlaps fully (4 of 4, all `base` tier). Any prebuilt set chosen on calibration therefore cannot help on test in F1/F2/F3/F5, and the receipt's per-family numbers show exactly that (greedy0/support/lookahead <= 1.0 in every family except F4, where they are about 2.04-2.08). The pooled gain of these policies is F4. They do not measure the quality of the selection rules.
- Pooling is by raw milliseconds: F3 (26.6 s) and F4 (18.0 s) are 89% of direct time. On-demand retrieval is the best or equal-best honest policy in F1 (1.499 vs Bayes 1.165), F2 (1.982 vs 1.477), F4 (tie) and F5 (1.096 vs 1.000); it loses only in F3 (0.296, break-even 91 > session 20). "It builds every needed lemma and loses" is true only pooled and only because of F3; this is the amortization law again (break-even < 20 in four families), not a defect of retrieval.
- The "privileged best prebuilt set" is not an upper bound. `oracle_best` searches subsets of size <= 6, and <= 3 when the pool exceeds 14 lemma units (F1 16, F3 25). In F1 retrieval-on-demand (1.499) beats the "oracle" (1.384). The label invites a misreading as an optimum.
- The library has 27 units in a tier named `test_ref`, 33 in `cal` and 7 in `base`; all are authored by the team, and the `test_ref` name suggests they were written as reference lemmas for the test templates (the draft never mentions the tiers). Retrieval/Bayes/oracle policies are therefore given a library that covers the test set at zero discovery cost. The invention result in the same section (0/400 hits on fresh F1/F2 targets) shows the authored coverage is not reproducible by the invention method. This contrast is not drawn in the text.
- Lookahead-2/3 (identical to each other everywhere) are worse than greedy-0 (1.102 vs 1.195), which is a calibration-overfitting signal worth a sentence; it is reported without comment. The draft concedes "descriptive, with no interval", which is honest, but still ranks six policies from five sessions.

From `vad_12_gate.json`: in-distribution `trm` 0.114 < greedy-0 0.119, `memoryless` 0.076 and `trm_oracle_features` 0.064 < greedy-0 0.119, `ff` 0.283 < `direct` 0.404; fresh_wide `trm` 0.114 < greedy-0 0.118; fresh_complementary every neural arm (0.50-1.90) beats `direct` (4.29). ### M4. "The exact belief state is supplied as features, which makes the problem Markov" is not what the code supplies (OVERSTATEMENT; biases against the neural arms)
Sentences (3.3 and 4(6)): "the exact belief state is supplied as features, which makes the problem Markov"; "the belief state is an input".
`scripts/vad_agenda_families.py::features` gives each edge slot 14 numbers: marginal existence probability, cost, conclusion reward, conclusion future mass, verified flags, four static structure numbers, budget/6. It does **not** supply N (future problem count), the deployment cap, lambda, the posterior over worlds (so correlation is lost in the correlated training family), experiment likelihoods (experiment slots carry only cost and budget), downstream edge probabilities/costs, or the status of sibling premises. The fresh complementary family has cap 9 vs 12-14 in training, so the controller cannot even see that the complementary lemmas matter. Greedy-1/2 see the whole instance. So (a) the features are not a sufficient statistic, (b) "memory does not help because the problem is Markov" is an unsupported explanation (memory is unhelpful, but also the feature set is lossy), and (c) the gate failure is confounded with feature insufficiency and with training on 1,000 instances (see M5), not only with "tiny controller" capacity. The draft's Limitations sentence "trained on exact-oracle labels from the same distribution family" is also inaccurate: the fresh families are structurally different from training by design (that is the point of the gate), and the training distribution contains no planted pair or chain.

### M5. VAD-12 design details that matter and are not disclosed (OVERSTATEMENT of what the gate shows)
- "6,000 exact-oracle decision sequences" are 1,000 instances (seeds 90000-90999) times 6 behaviour rollouts (2 optimal, 1 greedy-0, 3 random; `gen_data.py`), 18,532 decision steps. The effective instance count is 1,000.
- The training label is the optimal action with ties resolved toward STOP (`Oracle.best_action` keeps the earlier action), a systematic label bias toward stopping when Q ties (all zero-gain attempts).
- Checkpoints are selected by calibration action accuracy, not regret; `ff` has the highest accuracy (0.868) and the worst in-distribution regret (0.283).
- Two seeds, seed variance as large as inter-arm differences (e.g. `trm` in-distribution regret 0.066 vs 0.162; chain 0.582 vs 0.122; `shuffled_memory` regret differs from `trm` by up to 0.09 on complementary), so "shuffled-memory regret equals `trm` regret" and "memoryless matches `trm`" are "indistinguishable at 2 seeds", not equalities.
- `trm_oracle_features` receives the greedy-0 one-step net gain as a feature (`oracle_features=True`), is never defined in the text, and still has higher regret than greedy-0 on wide (0.146 vs 0.118) and complementary (0.501 vs 0.189). That is the cleanest evidence the failure is the learner/training recipe, not information, and it is not discussed.
- The shuffled-memory ablation indexes `bank[step]`, the hidden state after processing step `step` in calibration episodes, whereas the state fed into step `step` should be `bank[step-1]` (off by one; the substituted state is still another episode's state, so the ablation is probably still meaningful but not as specified).
- The generators for the fresh families were written by the authors after Proposition 3 (chains of length 3 so greedy-2 is exactly optimal; greedy-0/1 regret 2.25/2.41 by construction). The gate "within 0.01 of greedy-2 where greedy-2 has regret exactly 0" is equivalent to "be optimal", on instances small enough for the exact oracle (opt solve about 13-144 ms). The draft says this; it should then not describe the comparator set as "strong symbolic baselines" in the abstract as if the gate were a fair contest.
- Cost sentence: "about 3.9 ms per neural decision ... against 14-159 ms per instance (several decisions) for greedy-1/2 ... so the neural controllers are cheaper per call but not at matched utility." From `results.json`: the symbolic seconds are per instance of a full tree walk (about 7-9 decision nodes), so per node greedy-2 is about 3-17 ms. Neural per instance (trm 2.7-6.2 ms/decision times about 7.2-9.2 decisions) is about 20-46 ms, i.e. no cheaper than greedy-1 (16 and 14 ms) and about equal to greedy-2 (19 and 25 ms) on the complementary and chain families. Neural time includes feature computation (which calls `orc.terminal`) but symbolic time includes the evaluator's tree walk. The comparison is not like-for-like and the "cheaper" claim does not hold on two of four families.
- "Pre-declared gate": the gate rule first appears in commit a40cb84 (draft + receipt together); the LoopedTransformers commit 181bb78c (14:52) contains code, results and README in one commit and `train_eval.py` contains no gate rule. Nothing in the repository pre-dates the result. If a pre-declaration exists (e.g. in the task prompt) it is not an artifact a reader can check. Abstract/4(6)'s "pre-declared" is unverifiable.

### M6. "Each hypothesis is necessary" for Proposition 2 is not demonstrated (OVERSTATEMENT)
Sentence (2.7): "Each hypothesis is necessary: unequal costs ..., complementary lemmas ..., a chain ..., correlated edges make greedy-0 optimal in only 70% of 60 seeded instances, and a misleading heuristic signal reverses the value of trusting feedback."
- The correlated-edge counterexample uses the layered generator, which already breaks "premises among the facts"; layered instances without correlation give greedy-0 optimal in 78.7% (150 seeds; reproduced). The 70% therefore does not isolate independence. I tested that independence alone matters (`prop2.py`, modular structure, two latent regimes): greedy-0 is suboptimal in 46/1,500 instances (3.1%), e.g. seed 83 loses 0.23. So the hypothesis is necessary, but the cited evidence does not show it.
- No example is given for "distinct conclusions" or "no experiments". I found none in 4,000 random substitute-route instances (`prop2c.py`, 3-5 equal-cost independent edges onto 2-3 vertices, lambda in {0,1/2,1}, budgets 2-4) and 1,500 independent-edge instances with an experiment (`prop2b.py`): 0 violations. Those two hypotheses may not be necessary (or my search is too weak); the draft should not assert they are. The misleading-signal example is about trust, not a greedy-0 failure.
- Strategic-intermediate sentence: "even at probability 1 pursuing the target directly is strictly worse (-18 versus -14.8) because it forgoes M's reuse value": the optimal first action at p=1 **is** the direct edge (`spot10.py`: `('att', 0)`); the -18 is the `direct` baseline policy that stops after the target, while the optimal policy takes the target and then M. The sentence supports "the direct baseline is bad", not "a zero-reward lemma is the best action", which holds only for p <= 31/40 (verified).

### M7. Propositions 2 and 3: Proposition 2 survives; Proposition 3 has an unstated tie-break and parameter dependence (OVERSTATEMENT / gap)
- Proposition 2: I tried to break it with `prop2.py`: 3,000 random instances with 1-3 facts, multi-premise edges (premises any subset of facts), 2-5 equal-cost edges (cost 1-3), probabilities including 1, lambda in {0, 1/2, 1}, random N, cap in {3, 5, 12, 20}, budgets not multiples of the cost, mu mass on facts and on non-edge vertices. Greedy-0 value equals the exact optimum in all 3,000. I read the proof and found no gap in the additive-T argument. DEFENSIBLE. Minor: the proof sets D_v^0 = min(cap, c + |premises|) which presumes every premise is a fact with D = 1; that is the hypothesis but should be stated.
- Proposition 3: "greedy-j attains V iff j >= k-1" and "V = p^k R - 1". (i) The "iff" is tie-break dependent. With lambda = 0 every attempt before the last has Q equal to the stop value; `best_action` keeps STOP on ties, so greedy-j with j < k-1 stops. If ties go to attempts (`prop3.py`, reversed order), greedy-0, -1, -2 all attain V on a k=4 chain. The proof's own step ("Q_j of the first attempt equals the stop value") is exactly a tie. (ii) V = p^k R - 1 needs lambda = 0, N = 1 and all future mass on a fact (the builder's setup). With lambda = 1/10, k = 3 the value is 6.019, not 6.29. The Appendix states "T_0 = -1" as if general. (iii) Needs budget >= k and p, R > 0. The note's "verified exactly for k = 2..6" is true for those settings (I re-verified k = 2..5, and greedy-j == V exactly for j >= k-1 only). Suggested fix: add "with ties broken toward stopping" and "lambda = 0" to the statement, or add a strict epsilon cost.
- Proposition 1 is a telescoping identity, correctly proved; the in-code assertion is tautological given the definitions (fine, but it is not a test of the model).

### M8. VAD-09D: attribution of the 12.9 and of the 8.93 (CONFIRMED ERROR in attribution)
Sentences: "(iii) the planner's advantage over myopic filtering (12.9 utility over three families for 119,709 Q-evaluations) is paid for as long as one Q-evaluation costs less than about 1.1e-4" and "the law matters only through how quickly cues reveal the mode (8.93 for a law-aware planner)".
- The 12.9 = 40.181 - 27.295 is the gap between the **probe-using optimum** and a myopic filter that never probes. The no-probe planning advantage is 31.255 - 27.295 = 3.96. 8.93 of the 12.9 is the value of having an information action in the menu, not of planning depth. A cheap heuristic (probe the mode once, then filter myopically) was not evaluated in the draft; I ran it (addendum): it attains 40.18075, exactly the full optimum. So the planning advantage over a probe-allowed myopic policy is zero and "planning pays 1.1e-4 per Q-evaluation" is not supported as a statement about planning. CONFIRMED ERROR in the attribution.
- The "planner told the law" value 40.184 comes from `laws_known_dynamic`, which passes the full action set including the **mode probe** (probes are not filtered out). So 40.184 vs the no-probe 31.255 = 8.93 is again the probe's value, and the law's own contribution on top of the probe planner is 40.184 - 40.181 = +0.003. With probes removed the law-known planner is worth 32.224, i.e. +0.97 over the no-probe planner. The text "knowing the law adds nothing once the mode is known" is correct (53.430 both), but the parenthetical "(8.93 for a law-aware planner)" is misattributed.
- "knowing the mode is worth 22.2 over the best unaided planner": 22.2 is vs the no-probe planner; vs the planner that may probe it is 13.25.

### M9. Section 4(1) "wins at long [horizons]" contradicts the paper's own results (OVERSTATEMENT)
Sentence: "In every cost model tried (GF(2) caches, conjunction and Horn gyms, measured Lean work) a cached verified lemma loses at short horizons and wins at long ones."
