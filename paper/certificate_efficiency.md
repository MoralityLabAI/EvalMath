# Certificate efficiency under certified cost bounds (ZKL-15 crosswalk)

Evidence: algebraic identity and finite verified examples (17,710 interval cases, 12 focused tests). No neural or causal experiment, no sealed roots opened. Companion implementation: DataGoblin PR 67 and zk-rusto-no-bitcoino PR 34.

Let C* be the least positive integer certificate cost for a specified root, grammar, environment and cost profile. For a checked candidate costing C, independent bounds satisfy 1 <= L <= C* <= U and C* <= C. Consequently eta=C*/C lies in [L/C, min(U,C)/C]. If no checked upper bound exists, the candidate itself supplies C. Division by positive C proves the interval; exact reference bounds collapse it to a point. This is arithmetic conditional on the correctness of the bound and proof receipt, not a proof that arbitrary supplied numbers are sound. Invalid, missing, and budget-exhausted candidate certificates contribute zero. Report the mean of interval endpoints over all registered roots and separately report solve coverage. A capped *reference* optimizer does not establish an exact optimum: carry its admissible lower bound and checked upper bound.

Cost is a particular versioned function, e.g. unit distinct proof steps or an explicitly measured circuit-shape constraint profile. Do not equate a unit-cost DAG with minimal Lean/ZFC proof length; avoid comparing scores across incompatible cost profiles. Root identity must bind the theorem, environment, assumptions and task version. Checker custody and optimizer bound provenance are mandatory upstream responsibilities; the pure arithmetic module does not authenticate externally supplied claims. A frozen manifest must include every registered root, including failures (the scorer cannot infer omitted roots). No statistical or learned-mechanism claim is made by this deterministic module. Future neural evaluations require independent root units, a preflight power plan and sealed confirmation under the repository skill.
