# VAD-03A: bounded lookahead by fixed-coordinate signatures

Status 2026-11-25: completed finite exact synthetic control. Extends VAD-02; no trained controller, kernel proof, or general mathematical abstraction discovery claim.

## Indexed pair proposal

For nonempty development set T in GF(2)^d let I be its bitwise intersection, U its bitwise union, and F=I OR complement(U), within dimension d. A candidate pair (a,b) agrees with every development root on every constant coordinate iff

    (a XOR b) AND F = I.

Proof: every constant-one coordinate belongs to I, every constant-zero coordinate lies outside U, and every remaining coordinate varies. Define s(a)=a AND F. Masking distributes over XOR, so the condition is equivalent to s(b)=s(a) XOR I. Index candidates by s and query the complementary bucket. Each compatible unordered pair occurs once when retaining only j>i. Under expected constant-time hash operations and fixed-width masks the bounded enumeration has O(|T|+M+K) index work for M candidates and a cap K on emitted pairs: every bucket entry inspected is either a newly emitted pair, a reversed previously emitted pair, or a self entry (at most M). The claim concerns the stated operation model, not worst-case adversarial hash runtime, physical compute, or all useful pairs. The filter can omit useful abstractions and accept irrelevant ones.

The pair proof cost is exact: 2 min(wt(t),1+wt(t XOR a),1+wt(t XOR b),2+wt(t XOR a XOR b)); construction charges the cheaper of the two possible orders. A shared singleton sweep, bounded random search (same maximum eight pair evaluations), signature search (at most eight) and full pair enumeration share a candidate pool and development roots. Every method may choose the empty library. Maximize 1,024 times mean development savings less library construction cost; break ties by fewer caches then lexicographic masks. The selected library is fixed for every deployment horizon. Charge every residual probe for singleton and pair scores (17 and 38 units respectively at eight roots), including construction-order probes, plus signature intersection/union, mask and hash work, bucket scans, random rank sampling and unranking, or exhaustive pair enumeration. Cost units are declared abstract operations. Shared baseline computation, data generation, candidate invention, sorting, physical model/kernel costs and memory are outside this model; therefore the measured ratio is not a wall-clock speedup. A supplied pool includes the planted pair even under null and shifted controls. Random decoys are five-bit masks from the entire space, distinct within each pool.

## Finite experiment

Use 28 coordinates, pool sizes 16, 32, 64, 32 public seeds per regime and size, eight development roots, and 1,024 distinct deployment roots disjoint from development. A complementary planted pair contains a three-bit nuisance term and two disjoint halves of a four-bit motif; their XOR cancels the nuisance. Shared targets contain the motif and exclude nuisance; shifted targets contain a disjoint motif and exclude original motif and nuisance; null roots are unrestricted weight-eight samples; noisy-shared development includes six clean roots and two random weight-eight roots (in that order, without disclosing which are noise to the selector), with clean held-out roots. All targets have weight eight. Public seeds and nested deployment horizons are correlated, not independent confirmatory experiments. The initial dimension-24 configuration lacked sufficient shifted roots for the proposed horizon; it was corrected to 28 before producing the receipt and a capacity guard was added.

At M=64, H=1024, the shared signature arm recovers the planted pair in 31/32 families, scoring 106 pairs total compared to 64,512 for exhaustive search and 256 for bounded random search. Total charged selection work is 47,725 versus 2,550,784 for exhaustive (53.45x reduction) and 46,588 for random. All 32 shared families are profitable, with pooled total 440,791 versus baseline 524,288 (1.1894x). Exhaustive selection recovers the pair in 31/32 but costs 2,943,852 (0.1781x gain). Random recovers 0/32 and costs 541,218 (0.9687x gain). Singleton search costs 529,684 (0.9898x gain). Shifted and null signature arms yield 0/32 profitable families, with gains 0.9156x and 0.9164x. Under noisy development signature recovery falls to 3/32 and gain to 0.9804x, while exhaustive recovers 29/32 at prohibitive cost. In these controls the symbolic shortcut depends on stable coordinates and a supplied candidate pool. Pair recovery is a diagnostic; total charged cost is the primary measure.

## VAD-03B prospective robust controller gate

Before a neural run, add robust symbolic controls: a majority or confidence-threshold signature, a bounded multi-index over development subsets, and a beam with single- and two-cache actions. Fit threshold, number of indices and budget on disjoint calibration families, then freeze and evaluate on fresh sealed seeds. Include different corruption rates and noncontiguous corruptions, informative but shifted development, no planted pair, and several distinct useful pairs. Match total proposal work and verifier-call budgets, report actual costs including all index builds, candidate scoring, rejected hypotheses, training and inference separately. A recurrent controller can observe target coordinates, candidate features and development score feedback, choose whether to build or query an index, retain candidate pairs, stop, or abstain. Never feed planted labels or deployment outcomes to the policy. Require the learned policy to exceed the best robust symbolic arm in net gain on sealed families at matched closure and cost, including corruption and null controls. Test shuffled observations, memory removal and fixed-depth alternatives. Transfer to Lean proof states only after symbolic and neural finite controls pass, with source/target fidelity and dependency-leakage checks. No neural advantage is established here.
