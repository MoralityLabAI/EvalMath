# Mathematical capability beyond perplexity: a resource-bounded theory of reasoning controllers

Working paper v0.1, 2026-11-25. EvalMath EM-MPT-01--07. Analytic synthetic-model results and exact finite reproduction. Preliminary manuscript: no empirical model claims, no claimed priority over stochastic-order, POMDP, automata-minimization or robust-control literature; formal Lean supporting lemmas are candidates awaiting CI.

## Abstract

A predictor's held-out loss gives a useful measurement of average prediction quality. For evaluation of extended mathematical reasoning, proof organization, feedback, computation and persistent memory can change the probability of obtaining a verified result. We develop a finite resource-bounded proof-completion model in which these quantities are explicit. For independent geometric retries, a fixed product of obligation success probabilities admits sharp lower and upper completion curves at every budget. We derive exact comparisons with static quotas, introduce shared root difficulty and a charged ledger, characterize optimal Bayesian proof control, and compress a fixed policy into context-dependent residual-tree classes. We also give a two-world minimax calculation, uniform misspecification bounds and a confidence-envelope construction. Finite witnesses establish that the same designated-witness loss and the same predictor can support different completion probabilities. The intended application is an evaluation disclosure contract and a prospective compact-model benchmark, with separate evidence for measured inference costs and neural mechanisms.

## 1. Motivation and scope

Pachocki's [Perplexity and Intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/) (OpenAI, 22 November 2026) argues for cross-entropy measurements as a shared marker of progress and reports a change in mathematical capability at roughly 0.76 log-perplexity on its internal code evaluation data. The publication itself explicitly notes that inference-time reasoning can increase capability at unchanged pretraining loss. Anthropic's [Formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) (4 September 2026) reports an 11-day Lean campaign with roughly six billion generated tokens, where a maintained theorem DAG supported coordination, search and reuse after earlier attempts suffered memory and collaboration problems. These are motivating research reports, not evidence from a matched-compute scaffold intervention.

Let theta describe a predictor and its conditional outcome law, B be a charged resource ceiling, and Pi be a declared controller family. Define C(theta,B,Pi)=sup_{pi in Pi} Pr_theta[verified closure by B]. This notation makes the interface part of the quantity being evaluated. A loss disclosure requires additional assumptions to identify C. Even if one can estimate C on a fixed family of controllers, its value can increase with a larger interface. We distinguish base-model loss on a specified held-out corpus, conditional verifier success, and achieved end-to-end closure. The synthetic constructions assume a sound verifier; an empirical campaign requires independent certificate and theorem-dependency checks.

## 2. Independent proof persistence and loss-only envelopes

Assume d required obligations in a fixed topological order, independent attempts with obligation probabilities p_i, unit costs and irreversible successful certificates. With G_i independent geometric waiting times (infinite for a zero probability), closure at budget B is Pr[sum G_i<=B]. For identical probability p it equals the binomial upper tail. Fixed static quotas a_i sum to at most B; their completion probability is product_i(1-(1-p_i)^a_i). Coupling the first success locations proves static success implies persistent completion at equal cost. For d=4, B=8, p=1/2, persistent closure is 163/256, the best static quota gives 81/256, and a restricted failure-resets-all comparator gives 3/16. These are model results, not comparisons against every adaptive symbolic solver.

On the task-specific corpus containing one designated witness per obligation, mean negative log probability is L=-(1/d)log q with q=product p_i>0. At B=d, closure is q. At B=d+1, it is q(1+d-sum p_i). For two obligations with success probabilities (1/2,1/2) and (1,1/4), the loss is log 2 in both cases but B=3 closure is 1/2 versus 7/16.

**Theorem 1 (sharp loss-only envelope).** For B>=d>=1,

    1-(1-q)^(B-d+1) <= R(p,B) <= Pr[Binomial(B,q^(1/d))>=d].

Both endpoints are attained. The cases q=0, q=1 and B=d follow directly. At positive q, consider the formal generating function of failures before closure, q product_i (1-(1-p_i)t)^(-1). The CDF at k=B-d is the coefficient after multiplying by (1-t)^(-1). Replacing a,b by c,e at fixed product ab=ce changes that coefficient, for k>=1, by

    q(a+b-c-e) [t^(k-1)] H(t)/(D_ab(t)D_ce(t)),

where D_xy(t)=(1-(1-x)t)(1-(1-y)t) and H contains the other reciprocal factors. All coefficients after the prefactor are nonnegative. This follows from D_ce-D_ab=(c+e-a-b)t(1-t). Equalizing an unequal pair strictly increases every slack-budget CDF (at least one of the pair's failure probabilities is positive, so the relevant coefficient is positive). The compact fixed-product domain for q>0 contains a maximizer, which therefore must have uniform probabilities. Replacing two probabilities below one by their product and one strictly decreases the same CDF; after finitely many such exchanges only one entry can be below one. Its remaining B-d+1 attempts give the stated lower endpoint. For d=4,B=8,L=log 2, these endpoints are 289201/1048576 and 163/256. [MPT-01](math_proof_persistence.md) and [MPT-07](math_proof_loss_envelope.md) give the exact definitions, boundary cases and receipt.

This result is independent of the controller comparison: both extremizers use precisely the same persistent retry scheme. A token-level LM loss is generally not this witness loss, nor is an arbitrary proof-generation attempt a single designated-token event.

## 3. Correlation and ledger economics

Model a root-specific latent P with fixed finite support and iid Bernoulli(P) attempts conditional on that root. Cross-attempt covariance is Var(P); marginal success is E[P]. The closure curve is E[f(d,B,P)], where f is the homogeneous binomial tail. For B>=d and B>1, the interior curvature of f has sign (d-1)-(B-1)p. Thus heterogeneity can improve tight-budget closure and degrade it at larger budgets. For four obligations, the homogeneous P=1/2 curve versus the equal mixture P in {1/4,3/4} is 6.25% versus 16.015625% at B=4 and 63.671875% versus 54.325866699% at B=8. At B=12 the mixture value is exactly 11331581/16777216, and at B=16 it is 3425255975/4294967296. Equal marginal success does not equate conditional mean log loss.

Charge a deterministic C additional attempt units to the dynamic ledger, allowing B-C generation attempts. Equal-as-possible static quotas maximize their conditional product by the two-quota exchange inequality. At fixed known finite support S and exact mean mu, minimize the persistent-minus-optimal-static gap over feasible latent laws. The objective is linear in that law, so a minimum occurs at a vertex of the normalization/mean polytope. Every vertex has at most two support points: three distinct positive weights permit a nonzero mean- and mass-preserving perturbation. Evaluate all one- and two-point feasible laws to obtain the sharp robust gap and largest integer charge preserving a strict benefit. For d=4, S={1/4,1/2,3/4}, mu=1/2, maximum strict overhead is two units at B=8 and four at B=12; admitting both impossible and certain roots can eliminate strict guarantees. [MPT-02](math_proof_persistence_cost.md).

A deployment comparison must measure total wall time, tokens, accelerator work, verifier cost, retrieval, storage, policy evaluation and graph construction. The toy fixed charge is an incremental abstraction.

## 4. Feedback, policy memory and robust control

Replace P by a fixed hidden type z and stage/action success probabilities p(i,a,z), with positive integer costs c(i,a). Success advances the obligation; failure preserves progress. For unnormalized posterior w, the Bellman optimum is the maximum over affordable actions of V(i+1,b-c,w*p)+V(i,b-c,w*(1-p)), including stop. Completed proofs receive sum(w) and other terminal states receive zero. Positive cost enables backward induction. Independent alpha-vector enumeration checks the optimum. In the two-type fixture of [MPT-03](math_proof_controller.md), strategy history raises closure from the exhaustive optimal clock/progress-only value 199/256 to 205/256. This 2.34-point difference is below the proposed five-point practical effect floor.

For a fixed deterministic tree, define N(s) as the number of distinct specified residual policy trees at observable context s=(progress,budget). A controller reproducing the entire tree requires max_s N(s) mutable labels: distinct residuals at one context must decode differently. A context-indexed class label and transition table attain the bound. The selected optimal fixture policy requires one extra mutable bit, which is minimal even for expected closure because the zero-bit optimum is strictly smaller. Static table bytes, graph state and context encoding still cost resources. Larger fixtures require one or two bits for their selected policies; no global optimal-policy memory theorem is claimed.

An approximate posterior controller incurs regret equal to the sum of unnormalized Bellman gaps along its actual histories. The sum telescopes by cancelling child values; a stop contributes the entire foregone state value. At the normalized belief level, policy values are dot products with bounded conditional closure vectors, hence are 1-Lipschitz in total variation. Acting greedily at an approximate belief incurs a local gap at most twice the posterior TV error. This motivates learning a compressed action-sufficient state and auditing the accumulated error on deployment histories.

For two fixed possible latent types, the conditional payoff vectors of deterministic trees have a convex hull when the controller chooses a private policy lottery. The maximum of min(x,y) occurs at a vertex or a diagonal intersection with an edge. At most two deterministic policies suffice. In the fixture the deterministic maximin is 199/256 and the lottery between (193/256,217/256) and its reversal yields 205/256.

When the nominal and actual models share the action, transition and cost interface, an initial-prior TV discrepancy delta and uniform next-outcome TV discrepancy epsilon imply a policy-value discrepancy at most gamma=1-(1-delta)(1-epsilon)^H for at most H decisions. Couple initial types and next outcomes until the histories diverge, with the same policy coin. Optimal-value discrepancy is also at most gamma, and nominal-policy regret in the actual environment is at most min(1,2 gamma). Uniform error across relevant histories is a strong requirement; average calibration errors do not provide it. All statements in this section have their explicit contracts in [MPT-04--06](math_reasoning_memory.md).

## 5. Calibration and disclosure

For prespecified history/action cells with fixed-size iid Bernoulli calibration samples, exact equal-tail inversion and conservative grid rounding give individual confidence intervals. Bonferroni provides simultaneous coverage with preassigned alpha spending. For a frozen policy, assign each full-history node a valid conditional success interval [l,u]. Its lower value is min of p*L_success+(1-p)*L_failure over p in {l,u}; the upper value is the analogous max. Induction establishes valid bounds, sharp over rectangular interval models. Shared latent constraints can tighten these envelopes. Use [0,1] for uncovered reachable cells. Conditional samples collected with adaptive or selected histories need a justified sampling design.

Recommended EvalMath disclosure: (i) model/tokenizer/corpus and loss, (ii) controller family, graph access and certificate interface, (iii) charged resource budgets and measured cost components, (iv) conditional error distributions and dependence, (v) frozen policy selection and calibration uncertainty, (vi) independently sealed root-level closure outcomes. The loss metric remains useful. Its capability interpretation depends on this surrounding contract.

## 7. Prospective study and limitations

The [MPT-GYM-01 handoff](../research/math_reasoning_memory/MPT_GYM_01.md) specifies a CPU-first development pilot on independent theorem-generator worlds, with pre-declaration Lean environments, dependency-leakage sentinels, strong symbolic full-history controls, history reset and persistence interventions, explicit costs and a prospective preflight gate. It requires fresh independent families for confirmation, and frozen candidate selection, error spending and effect floors after development. A second development arm can vary the checkpoint at constant controller; comparing losses across checkpoints requires identical tokenization and corpus. A phase-transition claim would require held-out comparison with a smooth alternative.

This manuscript is a resource-bounded finite analysis. It does not establish actual loss thresholds, universal phase transitions, a neural causal mechanism, or that a smaller model can match a frontier model. No source-level novelty or independent mathematical referee report is asserted. The next experimental boundary is empirical measurement of the success/transition model and charged costs, followed by held-out evaluation. The sharp fixed-loss envelope gives a simple synthetic calibration target for that program.
