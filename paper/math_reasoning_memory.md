## MPT-04: Residual-policy equivalence

Fix a finite deterministic policy tree, with positive integer action costs and observable context s=(proof progress i, remaining budget b). Each node records an action (or stop), and the two residual subtrees for success and failure. Count distinct complete syntactic residual trees N(s) at each context, including explicitly specified off-support continuations. Two equal trees have identical actions and identical residual trees after each observation. The minimum number of reusable mutable memory labels for exact reproduction of this entire specified policy is max_s N(s); the bit width is ceil(log2(max_s N(s))). Proof: histories at one context with different residuals must have different labels, or deterministic decoding and updating would reproduce the same future tree. Conversely, assign one code to each distinct residual at each context and update to the appropriate child code. Context can change the codebook, since it is observed, and every transition decreases remaining budget. The result prices mutable labels, not static table size, context encoding, retained certificates, action selection compute, or costs of training. It is a fixed-policy minimization, not a global optimum over tied Bayesian policies. If one ignores impossible observation branches, further mergers may exist.

In the MPT-03 fixture, the selected optimal policy has width 2, and the exhaustive clock/progress optimum of 199/256 is strictly below the full 205/256. Thus one bit suffices and zero cannot preserve optimal expected closure in this interface. Five larger stress cases with 2--4 obligations and budgets 6--10 require 1--2 bits for their selected policies and 19--55 table entries. No global minimality claim for these stress cases.

For two latent types, approximate belief by a uniform grid with denominator g, nearest rounding with lower ties, and round after each online posterior update. A zero-likelihood update resets to 1/2 before rounding; this is part of the approximation policy, including off-support histories. At the MPT-03 fixture g=1,2,3,8,16 gives respectively 155/256,199/256,205/256,205/256,205/256. Grid state costs ceil(log2(g+1)) bits before policy compilation. More levels do not in general imply monotonic gain.

An exact regret certificate follows by telescoping true posterior Bellman gaps along any frozen policy tree. At each reachable history with unnormalized posterior w, subtract the value of the chosen action with optimal continuations from V(i,b,w). At a nonterminal stop the gap is V(i,b,w). Summing across the entire policy tree gives V at the root minus actual policy closure, because child optimal terms cancel and the remaining terminal terms are the policy's actual success mass. This audits actual online rounding, rather than assuming that successive Bayesian projection errors do not accumulate. For normalized beliefs, all continuation-policy values are dot products with vectors in [0,1]^Z; the optimal action value and optimal state value are therefore 1-Lipschitz in total variation. Greedy choice using an approximate belief with TV error e loses at most 2e locally (including stop as an action), so regret is bounded by twice the sum of expected reachable posterior errors. A one-step grid spacing alone does not bound cumulative posterior error.

## MPT-05: Minimax policies and misspecification

The alpha-vector enumeration from MPT-03 yields conditional closure vectors for deterministic policies. Nonnegative multiplication and addition preserve componentwise dominance, so dominated vectors can be removed without lowering any monotone objective. An ex ante random choice among policies produces a point in the convex hull. For two possible fixed latent worlds, maximize min(x,y) over that hull. A maximizer can be chosen at a vertex or on the intersection of a hull edge with x=y: split the hull by that line, optimize the linear objective on each half, and inspect its vertices. Consequently at most two deterministic policies suffice; enumerating all pure vectors and all pairwise diagonal intersections suffices. This assumes nature selects the latent world before the private policy coin is drawn. If nature sees the chosen policy first, the deterministic worst-case bound applies. The two-world restriction matters.

The fixture's deterministic maximin is 199/256; a half-half mixture of (193/256,217/256) and its reversal attains 205/256, which is also a uniform-prior dual upper bound. For all 225 grid cases the explicit mixture witness matches the minimum over prior breakpoints of the Bayes-optimal value.

For a fixed policy, suppose the nominal and actual model share action sets, deterministic costs, transitions conditional on the observed Bernoulli result, and the latent type space. Initial prior TV is at most delta, and conditional per-action Bernoulli TV is at most epsilon uniformly over every type and relevant history. Use a maximal initial coupling and couple each next outcome while histories and type agree, using the same policy random seed. Matching persists through at most H decisions with probability at least (1-delta)(1-epsilon)^H. Terminal closure indicators agree whenever the histories agree. Thus any fixed policy's closure discrepancy is at most gamma=1-(1-delta)(1-epsilon)^H. The true optimal value differs from the nominal optimal value by at most gamma by taking suprema over a common policy class; deploying the nominal optimizer therefore has regret at most min(1,2 gamma). This is a sufficient uniform calibration bound; observed average calibration error cannot replace epsilon. The 81 finite model-pair checks include prior and action-rate changes.

## MPT-06: Confidence-calibrated capability envelopes

For independent identically distributed Bernoulli probes in each predeclared history/action cell, invert both exact binomial tails at alpha_cell/2. For observed count k, reject low probabilities when Pr_p[X>=k]<=alpha_cell/2 and high probabilities when Pr_p[X<=k]<=alpha_cell/2. The probability of either tail rejection at the true p is at most alpha_cell by the discrete tail-test property. The module finds the largest rejected low grid point (or 0) and smallest rejected high grid point (or 1); monotonicity of the tails means rounding outwards covers the non-rejection region for every continuous p, not merely grid p. Bonferroni gives simultaneous coverage at least 1-sum(alpha_cell) across the prespecified cells, even if different cells' samples are dependent. Within-cell sampling must still satisfy the stated binomial law and fixed sample design, or use a separately justified sequential method. This is not a conditional calibration claim for histories selected adaptively by a researcher. For example 48/64 in one of four equal-alpha cells at total alpha=0.05 gives [0.59,0.88] on a 0.01 grid, and 96/128 gives [0.64,0.84].

For a fixed deterministic policy and the full observable history, propagate intervals [l_h,u_h] for the next conditional binary outcome. At a terminal node the lower and upper values equal its completion indicator. At an interior node take min over the two endpoints of p*L_success+(1-p)*L_failure, and max over endpoints of p*U_success+(1-p)*U_failure. Induction gives valid lower and upper bounds since branch masses are nonnegative. They are sharp over the rectangular family where the interval choices at each distinct history are independent parameters; actual shared latent models may yield a smaller attainable range. Uncalibrated reachable cells must receive [0,1], never be dropped or filled with an estimated mean. The implementation takes a frozen tree and explicit bounds; it rejects a missing cell rather than assuming any interval. A family of frozen trees can be bounded jointly if all their reachable cells are covered by the same simultaneous confidence event. A capability envelope for an arbitrary richer controller class would require bounding that class's complete intervention interface. No threshold for actual OpenAI or Anthropic checkpoints follows.
