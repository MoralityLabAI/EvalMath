# FAW-03: Bayesian experiment design with an unknown evaluation channel

**Draft 0.1, 2026-10-27.** Extends [FAW-01](agency_without_cryptomania.md) and [FAW-02](agency_persistence.md). The object is an exact finite stochastic control experiment about **evaluation-channel reliability**, adaptive calibration and generalization. No model, religious hypothesis, cryptographic algorithm, or real-world evaluator is observed here. All probabilities and costs in the executable verification use `fractions.Fraction`.

## 1. Protocol and quantifiers

One global, unknown reliability parameter \(q\) belongs to a *finite declared support* \(\mathcal Q\subseteq[1/2,1]\), with proper rational prior \(\pi(q)\). The true \(q\) is sampled **once at the beginning** and persists across epochs. At the start of epoch \(i\), sample a fresh independent fair polarity \(\theta_i\in\{0,1\}\). Every calibration or held-out item independently draws uniform true state \(s\) and Bernoulli noise bit \(e\) with \(P(e=0\mid q)=q\). The observed signal is \(y=s\oplus\theta_i\oplus e\). A calibration purchase reveals an independently certified gold label \(s\), providing the observed polarity \(z=s\oplus y=\theta_i\oplus e\).

Each epoch has a known number \(n_i\) of held-out predictions, calibration purchase cost \(c_i\ge0\), and within-epoch acquisition cap \(M_i\). The agent can purchase labels **adaptively** using all current-epoch calibration results and previous epochs' calibration history. It decides when to stop buying, then predicts every held-out state. Held-out true labels, verdicts and scores remain **sealed through the full horizon**, including between epochs. Unpurchased gold items and hidden states are unavailable. Planning computation itself has no charged cost. The rules and prior are known; actual \(q\), current \(\theta_i\), and noise are unknown.

This is a stylized gold-anchored channel audit. If gold labels themselves are corrupted, reused, selected after observing model behavior, or generated by a correlated grader, the likelihood and all conclusions about reliability require re-auditing.

## 2. Learning reliability needs two labels per fresh polarity

For a single observed calibration polarity \(z\), marginalizing the fair \(\theta_i\) gives

\[
 P(z=0\mid q)=P(z=1\mid q)=\tfrac12 \quad\text{for every }q.
\]

**FAW-03/A (one-label impossibility; algebraic identity).** One calibration item from each of arbitrarily many independent fresh-polarity epochs carries zero information about \(q\): the likelihood of every such sequence is \(2^{-N}\). With epoch polarities independent and fair, \(P(q\mid z_1,\dots,z_N)=\pi(q)\). An agent can still use the one item to infer its current polarity imperfectly and improve that epoch's predictions. The impossibility concerns **reliability identification**, not the current-epoch prediction utility.

For two labels sharing one polarity, let \(A=\mathbf1[z_1=z_2]\). Marginalizing \(\theta_i\) gives

\[
\boxed{P(A=1\mid q)=a(q)=q^2+(1-q)^2.}
\]

**FAW-03/B (identification with two-label epochs; algebraic identity).** On \([1/2,1]\), \(a(q)\) is strictly increasing. Hence each distinct rational \(q\) in a finite support has a distinct agreement probability. Two independently labeled examples per epoch, with refreshed polarity between epochs, make channel reliability statistically identifiable. The likelihood updates through the counts of agreement and disagreement from *independent two-label epochs*, never by treating different epochs' polarities as shared.

For a true support point \(q_*\), write \(a_*=a(q_*)\) and \(\delta_* = \min_{q\ne q_*}|a(q)-a_*|>0\). After \(N\ge1\) two-label epochs, estimate \(q\) by selecting the nearest support agreement rate to the empirical frequency of agreement. Nearest-rate ties select the smaller \(q\). A wrong decision requires a deviation of at least \(\delta_*/2\). Thus **FAW-03/C (finite-support consistency; probabilistic inequality)** follows from Chebyshev's inequality:

\[
 \boxed{P(\widehat q_N\ne q_*)\le\min\!\left(1,\frac{4a_*(1-a_*)}{N\delta_*^2}\right)\longrightarrow0.}
\]

The bound is worst-case and loose for small \(N\). It certifies consistency for every fixed finite separated support. The executable program also enumerates exact binomial identification probabilities on predeclared small horizons.

## 3. Exact Bayesian posterior and optimal acquisition

Let \(k_0,k_1\) count observations with \(z=0,1\) in the current epoch. The unnormalized posterior masses are

\[
 w(q,0)=\tfrac{\pi(q)}2 q^{k_0}(1-q)^{k_1},\qquad
 w(q,1)=\tfrac{\pi(q)}2 (1-q)^{k_0}q^{k_1}.
\]

Normalize by their sum \(Z\) on any positive-probability history. The probability that a new observation is zero, which also equals the probability that prediction \(\hat s=y\) is correct on a *fresh held-out item*, is

\[
 p_0=\sum_{q,\theta}P(q,\theta\mid k_0,k_1)
 \begin{cases}q,&\theta=0,\\1-q,&\theta=1.\end{cases}
\]

The Bayes-optimal held-out accuracy is \(A=\max\{p_0,1-p_0\}\). The conditioned oracle, which knows \(q\) and \(\theta_i\), has expected accuracy \(\sum_q q\,P(q\mid k_0,k_1)\). An agent choosing the wrong prediction can be caught by independently held-out labels; selecting calibration budgets on a reused grading set is outside the protocol.

**FAW-03/D (finite-horizon optimality; Bellman induction).** Suppose \(V_i(\pi;k_0,k_1)\) is expected *future* reward above chance, after prior calibration costs have been paid. Define \(\pi'\) as the marginal current posterior of \(q\). The agent may stop and collect the expected held-out advantage, carrying \(\pi'\) to the next independent-polarity epoch:

\[
S_i=n_i(A-1/2)+V_{i+1}(\pi';0,0).
\]

If labels remain, it may buy one more at cost \(c_i\), followed by a conditional branch:

\[
B_i=-c_i+p_0 V_i(\pi;k_0+1,k_1)+(1-p_0)V_i(\pi;k_0,k_1+1).
\]

Take \(V_i=\max\{S_i,B_i\}\) with stopping preferred on equality, force stopping when the cap is reached, and set the value beyond the final epoch to zero. The \(q\) prior is updated at the epoch boundary, while \(\theta\) is refreshed. Finite backward induction proves the planner is Bayes-optimal among all policies respecting these action and information boundaries. It does **not** imply computationally efficient planning for large supports, horizons or more general environments. No Lean formalization exists yet.

## 4. Complete finite witness: an experiment with zero immediate reward

Take \(\mathcal Q=\{1/2,1\}\) with equal prior probability. Each purchased gold label costs one unit. The preliminary epoch has zero held-out decisions and a two-label cap. Each of the following twelve epochs offers six independently held-out decisions and at most one label. All twelve polarities are freshly redrawn, while the true \(q\) persists.

The agent could skip the preliminary audit. Without prior reliability information, the single-label decision is worth

\[
6\bigl[\tfrac12\cdot 0+\tfrac12\cdot(1-1/2)\bigr]-1 = 1/2
\]

expected net units per later epoch, hence **6** over twelve epochs.

Buying two preliminary labels costs **2**, reveals whether their observed polarities agree, and changes future decisions:

| First-epoch observed pair | Probability | Posterior \(P(q=1)\) | Future action | Net gain per later epoch |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Agree | 3/4 | 2/3 | Buy one label | 1 |
| Disagree | 1/4 | 0 | Buy zero labels | 0 |

The expected future net gain is \(12[(3/4)\cdot1+(1/4)\cdot0]=9\). Subtracting the preliminary cost gives **7**, one unit more than the alternative. An immediate-horizon planner for the first epoch would refuse to buy any label because that epoch has zero test reward. The full thirteen-epoch planner rationally buys two. The first label by itself does not update reliability and has negative value when only one preliminary purchase is permitted.

The exact global Bellman policy buys an expected **11** labels in total: two for the first audit and nine expected labels over the remaining epochs. Skipping the audit uses twelve expected labels. Both strategies achieve **54** expected correct held-out predictions out of **72**, which equals the expected reliability-informed oracle accuracy for this support. Thus the full improvement from early experimentation is a lower *calibration cost*, with no change in population predictive success. The fixture is intentionally chosen to expose this mechanism with integer values. For general \(q\) supports, positive oracle regret and nontrivial policy errors remain possible.

## 5. Falsifiers and limitations

- **Polarity persistent instead of refreshed:** one label from different epochs may then reveal reliability, defeating FAW-03/A.
- **Reliability resampled every epoch:** the preliminary audit loses its value for future episodes; the initial optimum is to stop, giving expected future gain 6 in the specified fixture.
- **Untrusted gold labels:** the same posterior may estimate a mixture of grader bias, source-label error, and signal error instead of \(q\).
- **Leaked held-out rewards:** test feedback becomes training information, changing the Bayes information state and evaluation estimand.
- **Misspecified prior:** unknown reliability outside the finite declared support requires robust, continuous-prior, or misspecification analysis.
- **Price/label availability changes:** any costs, budgets or trial counts outside the declared fixture require recomputing the finite Bellman policy.

This is a *constructive functional learning witness* that never assumes one-way functions, NP hardness, or public-key cryptography. It cannot identify our actual complexity world, moral agency, consciousness, or what a creator prefers. The philosophical bridge between mathematical struggle and theological elegance is still an independent hypothesis. FAW-03 extends the existence result by adding unknown environment parameters and decision-relevant information acquisition.
