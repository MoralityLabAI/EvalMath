# Validity of a public e-process under the actor's filtration: leakage, anticipation and revocable provenance

**Draft 0.1 | 27 October 2026.** Exact finite results on the question left open by [stochastic full-filtration feedback](stochastic_full_filtration.md): when does a nonnegative process that is valid in the certified public filtration \(\mathcal G\) stay safe for stopping, query and revocation decisions taken in a richer actor filtration \(\mathcal F\supseteq\mathcal G\)? Module `src/spiral_ln/evaluation_dynamic_filtration.py`; tests `tests/test_evaluation_dynamic_filtration.py`; verifier `scripts/verify_evaluation_dynamic_filtration.py`; receipt `output/evaluation_dynamic_filtration/dynamic_filtration_v0_1.json` (23 checks, 200 random private-signal laws, 489 brute-force stopping-time comparisons, 24 control models, 150 revocation chronologies, 300 audit-state pairs, SHA-256 of the three sources). All arithmetic is `fractions.Fraction`. No model, grader, instrument or real stopping rule is run. **No Lean theorem is claimed**; Section 8 lists the obligations.

Evidence labels follow the repository convention: *algebraic identity* (proved for every parameter), *finite verified example* (exact on the enumerated cases only), *cited*.

## 1. Summary

Three answers to "which information must the evaluator possess, and which history must it preserve":

1. **Nothing beyond the public transcript, if the evidence is clean and the factors are pointwise valid** (Theorem 3). When each e-factor has null mean at most one *in every latent state* and the actor's private information does not anticipate the clean response it has not yet incorporated, the public e-process is a supermartingale in the *actor's* filtration, whatever private information the actor holds about the latent state, however it chooses its queries, and whenever it stops. Private information about the latent world is harmless; private information about the upcoming draw is not. Factors calibrated only to the public posterior predictive lose this immunity entirely.
2. **Otherwise, a leakage certificate** (Theorem 1). For any private symbol \(H\) with \(L\) values, every public nonnegative supermartingale \(M\) with \(M_0\le1\) and every stopping time \(\tau\) of \(\mathcal G\vee\sigma(H)\),
   \[\mathbb E[M_\tau]\ \le\ V(\mathcal G,H)\ \le\ \sum_h\max_g \mathbb P(H=h\mid G_n=g)\ =\ e^{\mathcal L(G_n\to H)}\ \le\ L,\]
   where \(\mathcal L\) is the maximal leakage of Issa, Wagner and Kamath from the public transcript to the private symbol, and \(V\) has the closed form \(\max_g\sum_h\max_{t\le n}\mathbb P(H=h\mid G_t=g_{\le t})\). The bound \(L\) is sharp, \(V=1\) exactly when \(H\) is independent of the transcript, and the certificate composes multiplicatively over rounds. The adjustment \(M/V\) (or \(M/e^{\mathcal L}\), or \(M/L\)) is therefore an e-value at every actor stopping time. This is a constant-factor alternative, under an information assumption, to the universal logarithmic-cost adjusters of Choe and Ramdas.
3. **Per-source provenance, if sources can be revoked** (Theorems 4–6). A revocation recorded *before* its source is viewed costs nothing; one recorded after is a post-selection whose exact worst case is computable and, for one fair bit, equals \(3/2\). The Bayesian belief state is not continuation-sufficient once revocation is in the future vocabulary: for a static world the minimal sufficient state is the tuple of per-source projective likelihoods (one simplex point per source instead of one); for a Markov latent state the \(2^m-1\) subset filters suffice, update exactly, and for two sources none of the three can be dropped.

## 2. Setting for private stopping

Finite horizon \(n\). The public transcript \(G_n=(g_1,\dots,g_n)\) takes values in a finite product alphabet; \(\mathcal G_t=\sigma(g_1,\dots,g_t)\). A private symbol \(H\in[L]\) is known to the actor from time zero (the worst acquisition time); \(\mathcal F_t=\mathcal G_t\vee\sigma(H)\). The joint law of \((G_n,H)\) is an arbitrary rational mass table (`PrivateSignalLaw`). A public process is a \(\mathcal G\)-adapted \(M\ge0\); it is a \(\mathcal G\)-supermartingale when \(M_t\ge\mathbb E[M_{t+1}\mid\mathcal G_t]\). Throughout, \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal G}\) and \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}\) denote the stopping times of the two filtrations, all bounded by \(n\).

## 3. Theorem 1: private stopping of a public process

**Theorem 1.** Let \(M\) be a nonnegative \(\mathcal G\)-supermartingale with \(M_0\le1\).

(a) *Decomposition.* \(\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}}\mathbb E[M_\tau]=\sum_{h}\ \sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal G}}\mathbb E\big[M_\sigma\,\mathbf 1\{H=h\}\big]\), each summand an optimal stopping problem for the \(\mathcal G\)-adapted process \(M_t\,\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_t)\).

(b) *Leakage bound.* \(\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}}\mathbb E[M_\tau]\le\sum_h\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_n)=e^{\mathcal L(G_n\to H)}\le L.\)

(c) *Closed form of the information value.* Over all such \(M\),
\[V(\mathcal G,H):=\sup_M\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}}\mathbb E[M_\tau]=\max_{g:\ \mathbb P(G_n=g)>0}\ \sum_{h}\ \max_{0\le t\le n}\mathbb P(H=h\mid G_t=g_{\le t}).\]
For \(n=1\) this is \(1+\max_g\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb P_H,\mathbb P_{H\mid G_1=g})\).

(d) \(V(\mathcal G,H)=1\) if and only if \(H\) is independent of \(G_n\).

(e) *Sharpness.* For every \(L\ge2\) and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) there is a law with \(L\) private symbols and \(V=(L-1)(1-\varepsilon)+1\); the leakage certificate equals \(L\) on it. The inequalities in (b) can all be strict: the PR #8 law (fair bit, private guess correct w.p. \(3/4\)) has value \(5/4\), certificate \(3/2\), alphabet \(2\).

*Proof.* (a) Let \(\tau\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}\). For each \(t,h\) there is \(A_{t,h}\in\mathcal G_t\) with \(\{\tau\le t\}\cap\{H=h\}=A_{t,h}\cap\{H=h\}\); replacing \(A_{t,h}\) by \(\bigcup_{s\le t}A_{s,h}\) keeps this identity (the left side is nested in \(t\)) and makes the sets nested, so \(\sigma_h:=\min\{t:\omega\in A_{t,h}\}\) is a \(\mathcal G\)-stopping time with \(\tau=\sigma_H\). Conversely any family \((\sigma_h)_h\subset\mathcal T_{\mathcal G}\) gives \(\sigma_H\in\mathcal T_{\mathcal F}\). Hence \(\mathbb E[M_\tau]=\sum_h\mathbb E[M_{\sigma_h}\mathbf 1\{H=h\}]\) and the two suprema agree. Since \(M_\sigma\) is \(\mathcal G_\sigma\)-measurable, \(\mathbb E[M_\sigma\mathbf 1\{H=h\}]=\mathbb E[M_\sigma\,\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_\sigma)]\).

(b) By (a) and optional stopping for the nonnegative supermartingale \(M\) on a bounded horizon, \(\mathbb E[M_\sigma\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_\sigma)]\le\mathbb E[M_\sigma]\cdot\max_{t}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_t)\le\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_n)\), the last step because \(Z_t=\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_t)=\mathbb E[Z_n\mid\mathcal G_t]\le\operatorname*{ess\,sup}Z_n\). Summing over \(h\) gives \(\sum_h\max_g\mathbb P(H=h\mid G_n=g)\), which is \(\exp\) of the maximal leakage \(\mathcal L(X\to Y)=\log\sum_y\max_x\mathbb P(Y=y\mid X=x)\) with \(X=G_n\), \(Y=H\); each summand is at most one.

(c) A nonnegative supermartingale with \(M_0\le1\) is dominated by a martingale with \(M_0'=1\) (Doob decomposition and division by \(M_0\); \(M\equiv0\) if \(M_0=0\)), so it suffices to take \(M\) a martingale, which on a finite tree is determined by \(M_n\ge0\), \(\mathbb E M_n=1\), a simplex whose extreme points are \(M^{g}_n=\mathbf 1\{G_n=g\}/\mathbb P(G_n=g)\). Each term \(\mathbb E[M_\sigma\mathbf 1\{H=h\}]=\mathbb E[M_n\,\mathbb P(H=h\mid\mathcal G_\sigma)]\) is linear in \(M_n\), so the objective in (a) is convex and its supremum is attained at an extreme point. For \(M^{g}\), \(M^{g}_t=\mathbf 1\{G_t=g_{\le t}\}/\mathbb P(G_t=g_{\le t})\) vanishes off the path of \(g\); the atom \(\{G_t=g_{\le t}\}\) is either contained in or disjoint from \(\{\sigma=t\}\in\mathcal G_t\), and the atoms along the path are nested, so exactly one time \(t^*=\sigma(g)\) contributes and \(\mathbb E[M^{g}_\sigma\mathbf 1\{H=h\}]=\mathbb P(H=h\mid G_{t^*}=g_{\le t^*})\). Every \(t^*\) is attained by \(\sigma\equiv t^*\). The \(n=1\) form is \(\sum_h\max(p_h,q_h)=1+\operatorname{TV}(p,q)\).

(d) The \(t=0\) term gives \(\max_t\mathbb P(H=h\mid g_{\le t})\ge\mathbb P(H=h)\), so \(V=1\) forces \(\mathbb P(H=h\mid g_{\le t})\le\mathbb P(H=h)\) for all \(g,t,h\); both sides sum to one over \(h\), so they agree, which is independence of \(H\) and \(G_n\). The converse is immediate.

(e) Let \(X_1,\dots,X_{n}\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((\varepsilon)\), \(M_t=\varepsilon^{-t}\prod_{s\le t}X_s\) (a martingale), and \(H\) the number of leading successes (\(L=n+1\) symbols). Along the all-success path, \(\mathbb P(H=k\mid g_{\le k})=1-\varepsilon\) for \(k<n\) and \(\mathbb P(H=n\mid g_{\le n})=1\), so \(V\ge n(1-\varepsilon)+1\); the module computes equality and the certificate \(n+1\). \(\square\)

**Evidence: algebraic identity** for (a)–(e); the receipt checks the closed form against the supremum over extreme points on 200 random laws with horizons one to three and private alphabets two and three, the per-symbol optimal stopping against a brute force over all \(\mathcal F\)-stopping times in 489 cases, the one-step total-variation form, the sharpness families for \(n\le5\), and the independence criterion on 60 product laws.

**Remarks.** (i) The decomposition makes the exact worst case for a *given* public e-process a sum of \(L\) finite optimal-stopping problems (`worst_stopped_expectation`); the closed form (c) is the information-structure value, independent of \(M\). (ii) With \(H\) the arg-max time of \(M\) (\(L=n+1\)), the private stopper obtains \(\mathbb E[\max_{t\le n}M_t]\), so (b) contains the elementary bound \(\mathbb E[\max_{t\le n}M_t]\le n+1\) for nonnegative martingales with \(M_0=1\), and (e) shows it is sharp. (iii) Ville's inequality is unaffected: \(\mathbb P(M_\tau\ge1/\alpha)\le\mathbb P(\max_tM_t\ge1/\alpha)\le\alpha\) for *every* stopping time, as [PR #8](stochastic_full_filtration.md) noted. The leakage adjustment matters exactly when the *expectation* of the reported e-value is consumed downstream: averaging, e-BH, products across studies, or the hierarchical alpha accounting of Beyond-Bayes ACE. (iv) Composition: for private symbols \(H_1,\dots,H_n\) arriving over time, \(\mathcal F_t\subseteq\mathcal G_t\vee\sigma(H_{1:n})\), and \(\sum_{h_{1:n}}\max_g\mathbb P(h_{1:n}\mid g)\le\prod_t\sum_{h_t}\max_{g,h_{<t}}\mathbb P(h_t\mid g,h_{<t})\) by the chain rule \(\mathbb P(h_{1:n}\mid g)=\prod_t\mathbb P(h_t\mid g,h_{<t})\), so per-round leakage certificates multiply. Later arrival can only lower the value (Theorem 2).

**Theorem 2 (arrival time).** If the actor's filtration is replaced by a coarser one, \(\mathcal F'_t\subseteq\mathcal F_t\), the supremum in Theorem 1(a) cannot increase. `sequential_private_value` computes the exact value when the private symbol's binary digits arrive at declared times, by backward induction on the product tree. *Finite verified examples*: in the doubling law (\(n\) fair bits, \(M_t=2^t\prod_{s\le t}X_s\), \(H\) the whole string) the value is \(1+n/2\) when \(H\) is known at time zero, strictly between \(1\) and \(1+n/2\) when only the first bit is known in advance, and exactly \(1\) when each bit becomes privately known only after it is public.

## 4. Theorem 3: clean-kernel immunity in the actor's filtration

The control model of the handoff (`ControlModel`): latent Markov state \(S_t\) with prior and transition kernel; public query \(Q_t\) chosen by the actor; clean response \(Y_t\sim K_{Q_t}(\cdot\mid S_t)\), with the responses of all queries pre-drawn independently each round; private symbol \(H_t\sim C(\cdot\mid S_t,Y^{\mathrm{all}}_t)\), so it may concern the latent state, the upcoming evidence, or both; factor \(f_t(Q_t,Y_t)\); \(E_t=\prod_{s\le t}f_s\). Timing *before*: \(H_t\) is seen before the actor stops or chooses \(Q_t\). Timing *after*: the actor commits, sees \(Y_t\), then \(H_t\). Two factor families: **pointwise**, \(\sum_yf(q,y)K_q(y\mid s)\le1\) for every \(s,q\); **calibrated**, \(f(q,y)=w_y/\mathbb P(Y=y\mid q,\mathcal G_{t-1})\) with \(\sum_yw_y\le1\), the public-posterior-predictive factor (a \(\mathcal G\)-martingale factor for every \(\mathcal G\)-adapted query policy).

**Theorem 3.** Let \(\mathcal D_t\) be the actor's information when it decides whether to incorporate \(Y_t\) (so \(\mathcal D_t\supseteq\mathcal G_{t-1}\vee\sigma(H_{<t})\), and \(\mathcal D_t\ni H_t\) under *before* timing). If for every \(t\)

(i) *anticipation-freeness*: \(Y_t\perp\mathcal D_t\mid(S_t,Q_t)\), and
(ii) *pointwise validity*: \(\sum_yf_t(q,y)K_q(y\mid s)\le1\) for all \(s,q\),

then \(E\) is a supermartingale with respect to the actor's filtration for every query policy, hence \(\mathbb E[E_\tau]\le1\) for every actor stopping time. In particular pointwise factors are immune to private information about the latent state under either timing, and to any private information received after the response it concerns.

*Proof.* \(\mathbb E[f_t(Q_t,Y_t)\mid\mathcal D_t,S_t]=\sum_yf_t(Q_t,y)K_{Q_t}(y\mid S_t)\le1\) by (i) (with \(Q_t\) \(\mathcal D_t\)-measurable) and (ii); taking \(\mathbb E[\cdot\mid\mathcal D_t]\) gives \(\mathbb E[E_t\mid\mathcal D_t]\le E_{t-1}\), and optional stopping on the bounded horizon finishes. For the two corollary classes: a latent-type signal satisfies \(H_t\perp Y_t\mid S_t\), and \((\mathcal G_{t-1},H_{<t})\perp Y_t\mid S_t\) by the Markov property and the fresh draw of each round's responses; after timing has the same conditional independence because \(H_t\notin\mathcal D_t\). \(\square\)

**Evidence: algebraic identity**, with the finite receipt confirming \(\le1\) in the two immune classes on 24 random two-state, two-query, two-round models (half static, half Markov), and confirming strict inflation in each of the four vulnerable classes: pointwise factors with a private pre-peek at the upcoming response (*data, before*), and calibrated factors with latent-state information under either timing or with post-hoc data information (which carries latent information to the next round). All values respect the control-form leakage certificate \(\sum_{h_{1:n}}\max\mathbb P(h_{1:n}\mid\text{transcript})\), the maximum over every query string and response string of positive probability; this certificate is policy-independent because conditioning on a transcript that records the queries cancels the policy, and it bounds the actor's value by the decomposition argument of Theorem 1 applied to \(\mathcal G_t\vee\sigma(H_{1:n})\) with the public e-process under each fixed public policy.

The PR #8 Section 4 counterexample is the calibrated/latent cell of this table: \(X\) fair is the latent state revealed noiselessly, \(f=2\mathbf 1\{X=1\}\) is calibrated to the prior but not pointwise valid. The immunity theorem says which information an evaluator need **not** possess: with pointwise factors on clean kernels, nothing about the actor's knowledge of the world matters, only that the actor cannot see a draw before committing to it.

## 5. Theorem 4: revocation chronologies

`SourceModel`: a composite null with prior over \(\omega\); source \(i\) yields \(Y_i\sim K_i(\cdot\mid\omega)\), independent across sources given \(\omega\); factor \(F_i=f_i(Y_i)\) with \(\sum_yf_i(y)K_i(y\mid\omega)\le1\) for every \(\omega\). A **chronology** is a sequence of events \(\mathrm{VIEW}_i\) (the value of source \(i\) enters the certified transcript) and \(\mathrm{REVOKE}_i\) (a slot at which the actor may declare source \(i\) inadmissible), each at most once per source. A revocation policy decides at each slot as a function of the values viewed so far; the reported value is \(\prod_{i\ \text{kept, viewed}}F_i\). `chronology_worst_case` is the exact supremum over policies (backward induction over the chronology); `revocation_policy_value` evaluates an explicit policy.

**Theorem 4.** (a) If every \(\mathrm{REVOKE}_i\) slot precedes \(\mathrm{VIEW}_i\) (`chronology_safe`), the supremum over policies of \(\mathbb E[\prod_{\text{kept}}F_i]\) is at most one. (b) Moving a revocation slot later in the chronology cannot decrease the supremum. (c) For a single source viewed then revocable the supremum is \(\mathbb E[\max(F,1)]\), which exceeds one whenever \(\mathbb E[F\mid\omega]=1\) for all \(\omega\) and \(\mathbb P(F<1)>0\).

*Proof.* (a) Order the sources by their VIEW times. Let \(j\) be the last viewed source. The indicator that \(j\) is kept is a function of values viewed before \(\mathrm{VIEW}_j\), because its only slot precedes its view. Conditioning on those values and on \(\omega\), \(F_j\) has mean at most one and is independent of the other kept factors, so \(\mathbb E[\prod_{\text{kept}}F_i]\le\mathbb E[\prod_{\text{kept},\,i\ne j}F_i]\); remove \(j\) from the chronology and induct. (b) A later slot sees weakly more values, so the policy class grows. (c) The policy "revoke iff \(F<1\)" attains \(\mathbb E[\max(F,1)]\), and no policy can do better since a revocation yields exactly \(1\). \(\square\) **Algebraic identity**; the receipt checks (a) and (b) on 150 random chronologies with one to three worlds and sources and the closed form (c) on 150 single-source models.

**Witnesses** (fair bits under a point null, factor \(2X\)): one bit, REVOKE after VIEW: \(3/2\); REVOKE before VIEW: \(1\). Two bits, both slots after both views: \(9/4\); the chronology VIEW\(_0\), REVOKE\(_1\), VIEW\(_1\) (safe): \(1\); VIEW\(_0\), REVOKE\(_1\), VIEW\(_1\), REVOKE\(_0\): \(3/2\). The explicit *cyclic cross* policy "revoke \(0\) iff \(X_1=1\), revoke \(1\) iff \(X_0=1\)" in the both-after chronology has value \(5/4\): no revocation consults its own source, yet the dependence is cyclic and the report inflates. A *triangular* policy (revoke \(1\) as a function of \(X_0\) only, never revoke \(0\)) has value \(1\); this is the acyclic case covered by the induction in (a).

*Consequence for the audit state.* The procedural machine of [PR #5](selection_risk_continuations.md) records whether an amendment followed a view; Theorem 4 says this chronological fact is the right certificate but must be kept **per source**: the audit log must record, for each revocation, whether that source's own value had already entered the transcript. A log that cannot certify this (the cyclic witness lives in a chronology where both views precede both slots) cannot certify validity, whatever each revocation's stated reason. The exact inflation of an unsafe chronology is computable from the declared kernels and factors, so it can be charged against the risk budget rather than ignored.

## 6. Theorems 5–6: revocable provenance and the belief state

Histories are sequences of (source, observation). Under the vocabulary "any subset of sources may later be revoked", the certified output after any continuation is the admissible posterior, so two histories are **continuation-equivalent** iff their posteriors agree for every admissible subset.

**Theorem 5 (static world).** With a fixed world \(\omega\) and conditionally independent sources, two histories are continuation-equivalent iff for every source their per-source likelihood vectors \(\ell_i(\omega)=\prod_{(i,y)\in\text{history}}K_i(y\mid\omega)\) are proportional on the prior support. The minimal continuation-sufficient state is therefore the tuple of per-source projective likelihoods (\(m\) points of the simplex), it updates exactly by coordinatewise multiplication, and it is strictly finer than the belief state whenever two sources have non-degenerate kernels.

*Proof.* The posterior for subset \(A\) is \(\propto\pi\prod_{i\in A}\ell_i\), so equal projective \(\ell_i\) give equal subset posteriors; conversely singleton subsets recover each projective \(\ell_i\) from \(\pi\odot\ell_i\). \(\square\) **Algebraic identity**; checked on 300 random history pairs. *Witness* (`static_factor_witness`): two worlds, two symmetric sources of accuracy \(3/4\); histories "source 0 says 0, source 1 says 1" and "source 0 says 1, source 1 says 0" have the same pooled posterior (uniform) and the same future predictive law for every query, but after revoking source 0 their admissible posteriors are \((1/4,3/4)\) and \((3/4,1/4)\). A state sufficient for current and all future prediction is not sufficient for future decision validity once provenance can be withdrawn.

**Theorem 6 (Markov latent state).** With a latent Markov chain and clean kernels, the family of subset filters \(\{\mu^A\}_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq[m]}\) (each the forward filter using only sources in \(A\)) is continuation-sufficient and updates exactly and incrementally, one forward step per subset. For two sources no proper subfamily is sufficient: `markov_factor_witnesses` exhibits, for a two-state chain with stay probability \(3/4\), histories with equal \(\mu^{\{0\}}\) and \(\mu^{\{1\}}\) but pooled filters \((21/26,5/26)\ne(5/6,1/6)\), and histories with equal pooled and \(\mu^{\{1\}}\) filters but \(\mu^{\{0\}}=(1/6,5/6)\ne(5/26,21/26)\). The per-source factorization of Theorem 5 fails because the transition mixes the factors between rounds.

**Evidence:** sufficiency is an *algebraic identity*; irredundancy for \(m=2\) is a *finite verified example*. Whether all \(2^m-1\) subset filters are needed for \(m\ge3\) is open. These results sharpen the Dot Matrix and PR #5 memory bounds from procedural bits to statistical state: separately revocable sources cost one simplex point each in the static case and up to one per admissible subset in the dynamic case.

## 7. Relation to existing mathematics

- Theorem 1(c) is a Bayes value-of-information computation in the sense of Blackwell (the stopper's decision is which of \(L\) public stopping rules to apply); its extreme-point argument is standard. The quantity in (b) is the maximal leakage \(\mathcal L(G_n\to H)=\log\sum_h\max_g\mathbb P(h\mid g)\) of Issa, Wagner and Kamath, equal to Sibson's \(I_\infty\); their operational reading (the largest multiplicative gain in guessing any function of \(H\) from \(G_n\)) matches its role here, and the chain-rule composition in Remark (iv) is elementary. The bound \(\mathbb E[M_\tau]\le e^{\mathcal L}\) under private stopping is, to our knowledge, not stated in those terms in the literature; we label it an elementary consequence rather than a deep result.
- Choe and Ramdas, *Combining Evidence Across Filtrations* (JRSS-B 2026; arXiv:2402.09698), lift a coarse e-process to a finer filtration by applying an adjuster to its running maximum, prove that adjusters are necessary among functions of the running maximum, and note the logarithmic price; they give a simulated example where a coarse e-process stopped in a finer filtration has mean above one. Theorem 1 is complementary: with an information-access certificate the price is a constant, and for a given process the exact worst case is a finite computation. Without such a certificate their adjusters remain the only universal remedy.
- Theorem 1(e) and Remark (ii) are partial-foresight values of a martingale, the discrete analogue of the "value of foresight" problem of Ernst, Rogers and Zhou, and sit beside prophet inequalities; the elementary inequality \(\mathbb E\max_{t\le n}M_t\le n+1\) is folklore.
- Theorem 3 is the standard argument that a factor valid conditionally on the latent state is a supermartingale under any enlargement that does not anticipate the draw; its content here is the exact separation of the four information classes and the explicit failure of calibrated factors.
- Theorems 4–6 are Myhill–Nerode-style continuation arguments applied to a stochastic state, extending [PR #5](selection_risk_continuations.md) and the Beyond-Bayes Dot Matrix bound; the per-source factorization is the classical sufficiency of per-source likelihoods under conditional independence.

## 8. Scope, assumptions and obligations

The private symbol in Theorem 1 is finite and its joint law with the transcript is declared; a physical acquisition system has to establish the leakage or alphabet bound separately, and a signature on a recorded value cannot do so (PR #7, Section 5). Theorem 3's anticipation-freeness is a causal assumption about acquisition order, exactly the *before/after* distinction of PR #8, and is not certified by any transcript. Theorem 4 assumes a point-in-time log of views and revocation slots. Theorems 5–6 assume declared kernels and conditional independence of sources given the latent state.

Lean obligations, none discharged: Theorem 1(b) on a finite probability space (decomposition of an enlarged-filtration stopping time, optional stopping, essential-supremum monotonicity of a bounded martingale); Theorem 4(a) as an induction over a finite chronology; Theorem 5 as the equivalence of two finite families of normalised vectors.

Next mathematical obstruction: a sharp *exact* value in the control setting (Theorem 3's vulnerable classes) in closed form rather than by dynamic programming, and the minimality of the subset-filter family for three or more revocable sources under a mixing chain.
