# PDI-04--06: robust audits, full-filtration risk and proof reuse

Evidence: algebraic arguments and exact finite synthetic checks; no model run, empirical calibration, or new Lean theorem. The prior stages define finite certificate availability, witness-backed authorization, and conditionally independent audit noise. The objective is certificate validity, not truth of the underlying mathematical theorem.

## PDI-04. Finite ambiguity sets and executable policies

A scenario specifies a rational prior and channel rates. All scenarios share claim families, channel identities, obligation indices and costs; the evaluator cannot observe the scenario. A deterministic policy is one common audit/authorization tree. Store its vector (f1,t1,...,fm,tm) of expected false and true weights, with its tree. The vector recurrence is PDI-03's Minkowski sum and union, now pruning only points weakly better in every scenario and strictly better in at least one. This is sufficient by induction because addition preserves coordinatewise dominance and each branch is chosen jointly for all scenarios. Every path meets its audit budget. The model assumes a static scenario and conditional independence of all potential channel outcomes given the world and scenario.

Independent randomization over trees gives their convex hull. For a robust expected-risk limit e, maximize z over nonnegative weights summing to one, subject to all scenario false coordinates <= e and true coordinates >= z. A vertex solution with k positive weights has k+1 unknowns including z, one simplex equality and k independent active inequalities among the 2m scenario inequalities; thus some optimum uses at most 2m trees. This follows from the finite LP vertex theorem (the optimal z is bounded, weights lie in a compact simplex); enumerate supports and active equations with exact Gaussian elimination. Complexity is exponential in the finite policy family. The returned trees and rational weights are executable; ties need not minimize worst risk among every optimal mixture. This is robust only against the declared finite set (and its convex hull over fixed scenario laws), not unknown or adaptive noise beyond it.

Witness: one fair validity bit and two unit-cost channels, budget one. Scenario 1 rates (sensitivity,false-positive) are (1,1/5) and (1/5,0); scenario 2 swaps the channels. The only nonzero Pareto deterministic policies audit one channel and authorize on positive: vectors (1/10,1/2,0,1/10) and (0,1/10,1/10,1/2). At e=1/20 neither meets both constraints. Half of each gives (1/20,3/10,1/20,3/10), and no mixture can improve the worst true weight because the sum of true coordinates is at most 3/5. The best feasible deterministic value is zero. These are synthetic rates, not estimates of a real evaluator.

## PDI-05. Conditional risk and revocation

Let H_i include all actor information before trial i, including private selection, and let E_i be the first invalid authorization at i. If P(bad_i | H_i) <= a_i on surviving histories and deterministic a_i in [0,1], survival obeys S_i >= (1-a_i) S_(i-1) by conditioning. Thus P(any bad) <= 1-product_i(1-a_i), with equality for independent Bernoulli hazards. Stops may be padded with zero-risk steps. For predictable random risk allocations, the union bound gives P(any bad) <= E[sum_i a_i], so a pathwise spending cap e implies an expected probability cap e. The sharper product formula is asserted only for deterministic bounds. Combining with PDI-02 gives E[false claim weight] <= E[sum_i D_i X_i] when the ledger counts unique false accepted obligations; an exposure cap C yields a uniform bound even for adaptively selected graphs. This is conditional on a valid witness-backed ledger and honest declared filtration, not a proof that a real verifier satisfies the hazard assumption.

Counterexample: two candidate audit outcomes are (1,0) or (0,1), each with probability 1/2, and both candidates are invalid. Each fixed candidate has false-positive probability 1/2. A private pre-peek selects the passing candidate, so its risk is 1. Marginal calibration alone cannot certify an actor-conditioned policy. The finite checker groups surviving histories by the complete pretrial signals and audits the maximum conditional hazard at each step.

For revocation, separate certificate eligibility (PDI-03's active version projection) from inference. A finite joint law over worlds and transcripts gives posterior by summing the mass of every cell matching retained coordinates and normalizing. Revoking a source's evidential license does not remove selection encoded in retained audit actions. A revised source reliability requires a revised joint law. In the two-source fair-bit example with independent 3/4-correct reports, transcripts (0,1) and (1,0) both give posterior 1/2. Removing source one leaves respectively 3/4 and 1/4. If its chosen action copies its report, retaining that action recovers the original posterior. If its revised reliability is 1/2 and the second stays 3/4, the action no longer provides information under the revised model. Thus one posterior cannot support all source revocations, and deleting a record is not equivalent to revising the likelihood. This is finite joint-law recomputation, not a dynamic causal-identification theorem or actual memory erasure.

## PDI-06. Synthetic workload and downstream transfer protocol

For N targets, a shared root costs R and a branch costs B. Fixed worst-case schedules with budget K can cover min(N,max(0,K-R)//B) shared targets or min(N,K//(R+B)) independent targets. The root is valid with probability q; a noisy root audit has sensitivity t and false-positive f; branches are independent valid with probability p and audited perfectly. The shared root is common, independent roots are fresh, schedules do not reallocate unused cost, and a target authorizes only if both root and branch pass. Expected true weight for k scheduled targets is k*q*t*p. Shared false-count law is a mixture: with probability (1-q)*f draw Binomial(k,p), otherwise zero. Independent false count is Binomial(k,(1-q)*f*p). The first moments match at equal k, but shared false counts have stronger tail concentration in the high-count event (for example all k falsely authorized when k>=2 and 0<(1-q)*f<1). The workload receipt checks the distribution against full independent finite enumeration. This is an explicit synthetic cost model, not evidence of neural transfer.

Prospective downstream experiment (public protocol, not a sealed dataset): freeze construction families by normalized proof dependency signatures, and assign entire families to train/calibration/held-out sets before model runs. Separate theorem statements from held-out lemma witnesses; provide only permitted verified artifacts. Compare no reuse, verified reuse, equal-budget retrieval, sham reuse of an unrelated lemma, and invalid-version/revocation stress. Record actual inference tokens, elapsed time, kernel calls, library search, amortized construction/verification cost, dependency incidence, unique theorem closure, proof replay, axiom audit, and false certificate weight. Use independent clean-room proof reconstruction, pre-declaration environments, explicit theorem identity exclusions and full dependency checks. Match total compute and verifier budgets, count all failed attempts, and cluster uncertainty by independent construction family, not downstream theorem. Freeze seeds, sample sizes, margins, stopping and analysis before unsealing; use EvalMath's mechanistic power preflight. Proposed minimum pilot: 32 independent families, four targets each, paired arms; pilot estimates variance only. No dataset is claimed sealed until independently held hashes and access boundaries exist. A downstream gym must run the actual compact-model experiments.
