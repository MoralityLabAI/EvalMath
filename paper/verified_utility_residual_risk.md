# NFAS-02: Verified utility versus residual failures
Research draft, 2026-11-25. Extends NFAS-01. Exact finite model and paper proof, no neural evaluation.

## Model and preregistration boundary
Two candidates in a block, mandatory quota one perfect audit. All candidates are provisionally authorized; the audited candidate is authorized only if it passes. The unaudited candidate remains authorized by the declared toy rule. Hidden failures X_i are independent fair bits. Values V_i are independent equally likely two-point nonnegative utilities independent of X and of the evaluation noise. Y_i = X_i xor independent Bernoulli(epsilon). Compare three order kernels: an independent uniform permutation; failure-first (fair tie break); success-first (fair tie break). Order can depend on X but never on values or noise. Known kernel for the calibrated optimum; frozen policy evaluated under the other kernels for misspecification. Grid: value pairs (1,3),(1,3) and (1,5),(2,6); epsilon 0,1/4,1/2. This grid is a declared exploratory census, not a prospective sealed empirical test.

Audit i gives verified good utility U=V_i(1-X_i), residual failures R=X_(1-i). A real approval policy need not authorize unaudited items; this deliberately models one that does. R is a count and here also a probability; U excludes the value of unaudited valid items. Neither metric is a measure of total deployed utility. A policy sees candidate identities. Online at the first arrival observe its identity, noisy bit, and value; audit now or reserve for the second. Offline oracle sees both noisy bits and values plus order, never X. Exactly 8 possible first observations, hence 256 deterministic policies; zero-probability observations have immaterial actions. Randomization yields their convex hull.

## Support-function theorem
For any finite observation W available when a decision is made, action a has posterior utility u_a(W) and risk r_a(W). For lambda>=0,
    
    sup_policy E[U-lambda R] = sum_w P(w) max_a (u_a(w)-lambda r_a(w)).
    
Proof: condition on W, take a convex combination of scores, bound by their maximum, and select an argmax in each observation. For two actions the maximizer changes only at nonnegative ratios (u_0-u_1)/(r_0-r_1). Thus all nondominated vertices of the randomized frontier are recovered by evaluating the breakpoints and open intervals, with minimum-risk tie breaking. Points on a face are obtained by randomization. When independent blocks are sequential and each has its own mandatory quota, the support function is additive: conditional on earlier blocks, the next block's law is unchanged. Induction gives the upper bound and independent local argmax policies attain it. Two identical blocks therefore double the single-block convex frontier. This does not establish online optimality when information can flow across correlated blocks or quotas are shared.

## Exact audit
Run node scripts/verify_nfas02.mjs --check. The integer weight denominator is 2048: independent latent and value masks, rational noise with denominator eight, fair tie order. The verifier directly evaluates each online policy on every world with nonzero probability and independently aggregates observation/action scores, checks exhaustive support maxima at seven prices and the offline upper bound, and checks fixed-identity audits. Exact fractions are stored in output/nfas02.json, linked to the verifier by its Git blob SHA.

Frozen transfer: select the independent-order online policy maximizing U-2R (minimum-risk tie break) and reuse its action table for both informed kernels. The robust deterministic frontier reports max across kernels of R and min across kernels of U for a single action table. It is a conservative rectangular guarantee, not the minimax expected scalar loss, and excludes randomized robust policies. No universal worst-order guarantee is claimed: only the three explicit kernels were enumerated. A follow-up may solve the exact mixed-policy linear program and allow order adversaries with knowledge of values and random seeds, matching the scope of the prophet paper.

## Structural side channel
At epsilon=1/2, Y is independent of X, but ordering leaks hidden failures. Always audit the first candidate: R=1/4 under failure-first (both fail), R=1/2 under independent order, R=3/4 under success-first (any fail). Always audit a fixed identity: R=1/2 across kernels. This holds even when binary feedback has zero mutual information about failures. The direction differs from NFAS-01 because audit selection here *removes* selected failures, whereas NFAS-01 measured failures among selected candidates. This is a full-transcript mismatch witness, not a violation of the conditional information bound.
