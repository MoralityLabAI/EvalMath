# VAD-06D/D2: dominance screening and bounded ancestor indexing

2026-11-25. Exact finite synthetic Horn reference-cost experiments; no new Lean replay and no neural training. Extends VAD-06C.

## Dominance certificate and limits

In the proof-tree grammar, a nonfact terminal goal g that cannot itself be cached requires a rule concluding g. Each rule reference and premise proof costs at least one. Thus 1 + the minimum arity of a rule concluding g is a lower bound for any proof, even with arbitrary reachable non-target caches. If a rule with only factual premises attains this minimum, the uncached proof attains the lower bound and no permitted cache improves that goal. The unary screen recognizes direct proofs from a fact in the nonempty-premise generator. The arity screen additionally recognizes two-fact proofs with no cheaper-arity alternative. These certify zero local savings, not zero information about future reuse.

VAD-06D uses eleven regimes, 132 calibration families and 264 fresh test families, disjoint public seed ranges. Every family has eight development goals and 256 deployment goals. Nine fixed policies and eighteen adaptive policies; calibration selects prefix_4 and unary_2_64 respectively. Adaptive screening observes until two, four or eight unscreened goals or a cap of 16, 32 or 64. Selection uses only observed unscreened roots. All observations are proved uncached; discovery, screening and construction are charged. The test baseline is 618177 reference units. Prefix_4 costs 439732 (1.405804x); unary_2_64 costs 448601 (1.378011x); arity_2_64 costs 465514 (1.327945x). All three use the same reference-cost convention; adding a common deployment proof-search scan charge gives gains 1.124548, 1.117630 and 1.104670. The adaptive gate fails. The arity variant rejects every zero-savings candidate in this test but costs more. In the unlock regimes an initially dominated alternate route exposes its hub before any proof savings appear, so discarding dominated goals sacrifices useful evidence.

## VAD-06D2: bounded ancestor index

Fresh generator and disjoint public calibration/test seed ranges: 14 regimes, 168 calibration families and 336 test families. The follow-up explicitly separates latent unlocks (hub already occurs in an alternate derivation during the easy phase) from concealed unlocks (no hub route until after 16 or 48 deployment goals). Deep routes add two unique wrappers around a common hub. Also include shared, mixed, shifted, late-shift, null, unary-dominated, binary-dominated, and intermittent regimes. Private derivations are longer than in D, so numerical gains across the two experiments are not directly comparable.

For a nonfact ancestor, record its shortest graph distance from the current goal by bounded breadth-first traversal. Count each ancestor at most once per goal, and maintain observed support counts, cumulative distances and hard-goal counts. A repeated ancestor is eligible when its support reaches the threshold and at least one of its supporting roots is marked hard; the all variant marks every root hard, and the hard variant uses the arity screen. When the first candidate becomes eligible, choose highest support, then smallest total distance, then smallest ID. Stop at the prescribed observation cap if there is no candidate. Candidate creation is legal only from the observed cone and the cached atom is derived from the original facts/rules. No later re-selection occurs.

Calibration selects ancestor_3_2_64_all among 24 variants and unary_2_64 among six baselines. Test baseline 976880; selected ancestor policy 608497 (1.605398x); strongest calibrated comparator 653833 (1.494082x); prefix_4 764255 (1.278212x). Relative charged cost savings versus the calibrated comparator: 45336 units (6.93%), with 312 wins in 336 paired families. A descriptive stratified family bootstrap (4000 draws) gives total-saving interval [39849,50789]. At a common DP scan charge, gain relative to direct is 1.154858x. Profitable in 262/336 families; selected in 312/336, including 48 permanently dominated families with zero savings. All 72 concealed/deep-concealed cases are profitable, but the 24 null cases are unprofitable and consume the full observation cap. The eight-goal development-only information is available to the offline comparator; the prefix-only policies use no development roots. All costs of the uncached prefix are included. Index creation, physical memory, measured compute and Lean kernel execution are outside the abstract charge; tests verify legality within the generated DAG, not a general Lean environment.

## Observation ambiguity

Suppose two deployment worlds share an identical observed prefix. In one world a candidate cache yields future gross saving S > c > 0 at construction cost c; in the other it saves zero. Consider the restricted decision of constructing immediately or abstaining permanently, with an omniscient comparator. The expected regret under randomized construction probability p is (1-p)(S-c) in the useful world and pc in the useless world. Minimizing the maximum gives p=(S-c)/S and regret c(S-c)/S. This is an elementary conditional two-action result, not a lower bound on all adaptive policies that can wait and observe. It explains why a certificate about current proof costs is insufficient to certify future utility.
